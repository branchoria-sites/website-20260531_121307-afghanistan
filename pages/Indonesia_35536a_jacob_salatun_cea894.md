## Introduction

Was [Indonesia]({{ 'what-indonesias-ufo-record-really-shows/' | relative_url }}) ever officially studying UFOs? The closest honest answer is: not in the way many people imagine, but closer than many countries in Southeast Asia. Indonesia never developed a publicly accessible government UFO archive, never announced a formal national programme comparable to the better-known American investigations, and never released a large body of military case files. Yet for decades, discussions of unidentified aerial phenomena in Indonesia were shaped by one unusually influential figure: Air Vice Marshal Jacob Salatun, a respected aerospace official and one of the founders of Indonesia’s national space and aeronautics institutions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Salatun" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jacob Salatun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jacob Salatun</span></span></span>

Because Salatun held genuine standing inside Indonesia’s aviation and scientific establishment, UFOs acquired a semi-official aura that they rarely achieved elsewhere in the region. His books, lectures and study groups encouraged the idea that UFO reports deserved investigation as possible aerospace and security problems. At the same time, his prominence created a persistent misunderstanding: many later enthusiasts assumed Indonesia must have had a hidden or formal state UFO programme when the available evidence points instead to a respected individual operating at the boundary between official aerospace culture and civilian ufology. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Salatun" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jacob Salatun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO: Salah Satu Masalah Dunia Masa Kini (1982). ## Salatun’s aerospace status gave UFOs unusual legitimacy

Jacob Salatun was not a fringe commentator. He was an Indonesian Air Force officer who rose to the rank of air vice marshal and became associated with the creation of the National Institute of Aeronautics and Space, widely known by its former acronym LAPAN. He also briefly served as Minister of Industry during the Sukarno era. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_sightings_in_Indonesia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO sightings in Indonesia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sightings in Indonesia</span></span></span>

That background matters because Indonesia’s UFO tradition did not emerge primarily from occult groups, conspiracy publishers or tabloid culture. Instead, one of the country’s most visible advocates for taking UFO reports seriously was a man with credentials in aviation, national development and aerospace policy. This gave the subject a degree of prestige that outlasted him.

Salatun argued that UFOs should be studied not simply because they might involve extraterrestrial visitors, but because unexplained aerial reports could intersect with aviation safety, national security and technological questions. In a communication cited by later UFO researchers, he stated that the phenomenon deserved serious study for “sociology, technology, and security” reasons. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reported_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">List of reported UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of reported UFO sightings</span></span></span>

This framing is important because it resembles the language now used in modern UAP debates. Salatun was not presenting UFOs only as proof of alien life. He frequently treated them as a category of unexplained aerospace events that could have practical implications if even a small fraction of reports were accurate. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dokumen.pub/ufo-uap-in-defense-and-security-perspective.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dokumen.pub">[dokumen.pub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dokumen.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO / UAP IN DEFENSE AND SECURITY PERSPECTIVESECURITY (AIR VICE-MARSHAL JACOB SALATUN) ❑UFO activities has been reported repeatedly distu...</span></span></span>

## The books that shaped Indonesian UFO culture

Salatun’s influence spread through publishing long before the internet era. His best-known UFO books include *Menjingkap Rahasia Piring Terbang* (“Revealing the Secret of Flying Saucers”), published in 1960, and *UFO: Salah Satu Masalah Dunia Masa Kini* (“UFO: One of the Present World Problems”), published in 1982. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucerThe shape became visual shorthand for alien invaders. These works became reference points for later Indonesian enthusiasts because they collected reports, discussed international UFO debates and attempted to place Indonesian cases within a wider global framework. Unlike many later online compilations, Salatun’s books appeared during a period when Indonesian aerospace development itself was becoming a national priority.

One reason his books remain influential is that they helped preserve cases that might otherwise have disappeared from public memory. Indonesian UFO history is fragmented: many reports survive only because they were repeated in later books, magazines or enthusiast archives. Salatun’s writings became one of the channels through which incidents from the 1950s, 1960s and 1970s continued to circulate. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Salatun" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jacob Salatun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO: Salah Satu Masalah Dunia Masa Kini (1982). This preservation role, however, also creates a historical problem. When modern writers cite an old Indonesian UFO case, the chain of evidence often leads back to Salatun or to later authors who relied on him. That does not automatically make the report false, but it means independent verification is frequently difficult.

## The Dwikora incident and the military connection

The case most strongly associated with Salatun is the so-called Dwikora UFO incident during the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation in 1964.

According to accounts later published by Salatun, unidentified objects reportedly appeared over parts of East Java for several days. Witness descriptions included unusual lights and a dark object sometimes described as mango-shaped. Some versions of the story claim military personnel attempted to engage the objects with anti-aircraft fire. The reports became one of the most famous entries in Indonesian UFO literature because they linked the phenomenon directly to a period of military tension. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://wartabulukumba.pikiran-rakyat.com/literatur/pr-876835794/tahukah-anda-pionir-peneliti-ufo-indonesia-adalah-pendiri-lapan?page=all" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wartabulukumba.pikiran-rakyat.com">[Warta Bulukumba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wartabulukumba.pikiran-rakyat.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Warta Bulukumba Tahukah Anda?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pionir peneliti UFO Indonesia adalah pendiri...1 Jul 2023 — Peristiwa &#x27;UFO Dwikora&#x27; tahun 1964, TNI menembaki piring terbang di Surabaya...</span></span></span>

For believers, the significance of the story is obvious: it appears to involve pilots, radar observations and military responses rather than a single civilian witness.

For historians, the problem is equally obvious. Publicly available documentation remains limited. The incident is known primarily through later retellings, UFO literature and secondary reporting rather than a large surviving collection of military records. The story therefore occupies an uncertain position in Indonesia’s UFO history: important, frequently repeated and culturally influential, but not supported by a transparent archive that would allow detailed reconstruction. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_sightings_in_Indonesia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO sightings in Indonesia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sightings in Indonesia</span></span></span>

That tension captures much of Salatun’s legacy. He preserved stories that might otherwise have vanished, yet the evidence available to modern researchers is often far thinner than the stories’ later reputation suggests.

## SUFOI and the emergence of a semi-official UFO tradition

Salatun’s influence extended beyond books. He also became associated with Studi UFO Indonesia, often abbreviated as SUFOI, an Indonesian UFO study organisation active during the late twentieth century. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reported_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">List of reported UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of reported UFO sightings</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thejakartapost.com/paper/2021/06/08/not-a-cult-ris-biggest-ufo-community-beta-ufo-weighs-in-on-cia-report.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thejakartapost.com">[The Jakarta Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thejakartapost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">not a cult ris biggest ufo community beta ufo weighs in on cia report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Not a cult: RI&#x27;s biggest UFO-community BETA-UFO weighs...8 Jun 2021 — Jacob, who was a vice air marshal in the Indonesian air force, pio...</span></span></span>

The existence of SUFOI is one reason some observers mistakenly conclude that Indonesia operated an official UFO bureau. The reality appears more complicated.

SUFOI benefited from Salatun’s stature and connections. Because its most prominent advocate was a senior aerospace figure, the organisation projected more institutional credibility than many civilian UFO groups elsewhere. Yet available evidence does not show that SUFOI functioned as a formal state agency with investigative powers, classified archives or a permanent government mandate. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucerThe shape became visual shorthand for alien invaders. During the 1960s, saucers waned in popularity as UFOs were reported...</span></span></span>

Instead, Indonesia developed what might be called a semi-official UFO tradition:

* UFO research was associated with a nationally respected aerospace figure.
* The topic occasionally intersected with aviation and security discussions.
* Civilian researchers could point to elite support for taking reports seriously.

## Why LAPAN and BRIN claims require caution

Modern discussions frequently exaggerate the relationship between UFO research and Indonesia’s scientific institutions.

Because Salatun helped establish LAPAN, later writers sometimes imply that LAPAN itself conducted official UFO investigations. Available public evidence does not support that conclusion. Salatun’s personal interest in UFOs is well documented, but that is different from demonstrating that LAPAN operated a dedicated UFO office, maintained a public case database or endorsed extraterrestrial explanations. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucerThe shape became visual shorthand for alien invaders. ## What Indonesia’s “official UFO history” really amounts to

Indonesia’s UFO tradition is unusual because it revolves around a respected aerospace insider rather than a large body of released government records. Jacob Salatun gave the subject visibility, intellectual legitimacy and a connection to aviation institutions that few Southeast Asian countries possessed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucerThe shape became visual shorthand for alien invaders.
