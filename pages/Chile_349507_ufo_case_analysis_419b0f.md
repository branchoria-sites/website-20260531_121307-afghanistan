## Introduction

The way Chiles unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) or UFO reports are **analysed and resolved** is as important as the raw sightings themselves. Unlike folkloredriven accounts, [Chile]({{ 'how-chile-investigates-ufos-and-aerial/' | relative_url }}) has maintained a **formal aviation review mechanism** originally the Comit de Estudios de Fenmenos Areos Anmalos (CEFAA) and now the Seccin de Estudios de Fenmenos Areos Anmalos (SEFAA) under its civil aviation authority to examine, classify and, where possible, explain unusual aerial observations. The focus of this report is not on retelling sighting stories, but on **how such reports are evaluated and resolved**, what kinds of evidence are brought to bear, how misidentification is handled, and what distinguishes **credible unresolved cases** from those that can be explained through common atmospheric or observational phenomena.

## How Photographic and Radar Evidence Is Evaluated

One of the central challenges in analysing Chilean UFO reports lies in the **quality and completeness of evidence**. Most reports come from eyewitnesses: pilots, military personnel or civilians who observe something unusual and often submit images or video. However, aerial photography and radar data vary tremendously in resolution and context.

### Strengths and Limitations of Visual Material

Photographic and video evidence is central to many CEFAA investigations. In notable cases, such as a 2014 Chilean Navy infrared video, recorded footage became public only after extensive investigation. CEFAA released a tenminute thermal imaging clip from a Chilean naval helicopter that showed an object matching the aircrafts speed and altitude, visible on infrared but not on radar, and reportedly discharging heat plumes. However, despite professional review involving radar logs, air traffic records and specialist analysis, no definitive explanation was found. CEFAAs report concluded that what was observed was a bona fide Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon largely because routine explanations such as commercial aircraft, birds, space debris, atmospheric effects or known flight paths could not be confidently matched to the data. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2017/01/after-two-years-of-study-chilean-officials-cant-explain-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arstechnica.com">[Ars Technica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arstechnica.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ars TechnicaChilean officials cant identify a strange IR signal seen by its Navy - Ars TechnicaJanuary 6, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2017</span></span></span>

At the same time, photographic evidence can mislead. Poor image geometry, lack of metadata, and the ease of optical illusions or misclassification make photographs less reliable without supporting data. In many routine reports, CEFAA has identified objects initially reported as UFOs as planets, satellites, aircraft lights, or even insects near the camera. One Chilean press interview with CEFAA officials noted that **the vast majority of public reports do not prove anomalous objects**: many involve ordinary objects misidentified as something unusual, including meteors, planets or even psychological misperception. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.publimetro.cl/cl/nacional/2014/07/03/cefaa-confunden-ovnis-meteoritos-planetas-o-bichos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publimetro.cl">[Publimetro Chile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publimetro.cl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Publimetro ChileCefaa: Confunden ovnis con meteoritos, planetas o bichos Publimetro ChileJuly 2, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2014</span></span></span> The reliance on visual material in isolation, therefore, is inherently prone to **false positives** unless corroborated with radar or independent measurement.

### Role of Radar and Flight Data

Radar verification and flight tracking are crucial in distinguishing known aircraft from genuinely unexplained targets. In the 2014 naval case, multiple ground radar stations and air traffic control were consulted with no detection of other traffic which elevated the incident to unexplained status. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://birdinflight.com/en/news/industry/20170111-chile-ufo-video.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: birdinflight.com">[Bird In Flight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">birdinflight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bird In FlightChilean Military Published a Previously Classified Video...11 Jan 2017 CEFAA experts study data about unidentified objec...</span></span></span> In contrast, many civilian sightings that are later resolved turn out to be aircraft, satellites or drones that do appear in radar or flight plan data once the report location and time are precisely aligned.

## Role of Pilot and Civilian Reports

Witness credibility is a major factor in how seriously a case is analysed. Chiles approach has consistently weighted **professional observers more heavily**:

* **Pilot reports:** Military and civilian pilots bring aviation experience and consistent sensory training, making their observations less likely to be simple misidentifications of clouds, aircraft lights, or astronomical bodies. The Chilean Navy incident noted above involved trained observers with consistent radio contact logs and video capture. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2017/01/after-two-years-of-study-chilean-officials-cant-explain-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arstechnica.com">[Ars Technica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arstechnica.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ars TechnicaChilean officials cant identify a strange IR signal seen by its Navy - Ars TechnicaJanuary 6, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2017</span></span></span>
* **Civilian sightings:** Reports from the public vary widely in reliability. They often lack corroborative data (radar, multiple angles, timestamps), and CEFAA has publicly acknowledged that many such reports turn out to be misinterpretations of ordinary phenomena. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.publimetro.cl/cl/nacional/2014/07/03/cefaa-confunden-ovnis-meteoritos-planetas-o-bichos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publimetro.cl">[Publimetro Chile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publimetro.cl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Publimetro ChileCefaa: Confunden ovnis con meteoritos, planetas o bichos Publimetro ChileJuly 2, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2014</span></span></span>

Despite credibility differences, **all reports are investigated using the same framework**: collecting all available evidence; consulting specialists (meteorologists, astronomers, engineers); and checking against known flight schedules and atmospheric conditions.

## Sceptical Review and Prosaic Explanations

A recurring theme in analysing Chilean UFO reports is how often initial impressions of an anomaly can be reconciled with ordinary explanations upon thorough review.

### Common Sources of Misidentification

Globally, studies of unidentified aerial phenomena find that most apparent UFOs are eventually explained as conventional objects or phenomena aircraft, balloons, satellites, meteors, or even bright planets and stars once additional data is analysed. For example, in extensive statistical reviews like the U.S. Air Forces Project Blue Book, the vast majority of sightings were reliably matched to known causes after careful crosschecking. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_studies_of_UFOs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Identification studies of UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Identification studies of UFOs</span></span></span>

In Chile, CEFAA has reported that many received cases fall into this category. Published interviews and official comments indicate that a large proportion of public UFO reports actually turn out to be:

* **Astronomical objects:** bright planets, meteors, satellites visible at certain angles.
* **Identifiable aircraft:** commercial, military or private flights misperceived under unusual lighting.
* **Optical or perceptual artefacts:** reflections, camera artefacts, or insects near lenses.

CEFAAs own director has highlighted that such misidentifications are a **leading reason why relatively few cases remain unexplained** after review. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.publimetro.cl/cl/nacional/2014/07/03/cefaa-confunden-ovnis-meteoritos-planetas-o-bichos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publimetro.cl">[Publimetro Chile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publimetro.cl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Publimetro ChileCefaa: Confunden ovnis con meteoritos, planetas o bichos Publimetro ChileJuly 2, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2014</span></span></span>

### When Explainable Explanations Fail

A small number of cases do resist mundane explanations for example, when multiple credible witnesses, clear imaging, radar crosschecks and weather data are all inconsistent with known objects or phenomena. In such instances, CEFAA and later SEFAA classify the case as an **Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon (UAP)** rather than asserting an extraordinary cause. The terminology reflects that an object is both **unidentified and currently unexplained** given the available evidence, rather than implying alien origin or other sensational interpretations. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comit%C3%A9_de_Estudios_de_Fen%C3%B3menos_A%C3%A9reos_An%C3%B3malos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comit de Estudios de Fenmenos Areos Anmalos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comit de Estudios de Fenmenos Areos Anmalos</span></span></span>

This distinction follows broader scientific practice: unidentified does not automatically equate to inexplicable in principle, only that **existing data and models cannot yet provide a confident identification**.

## Weighing Evidence Quality in Official Conclusions

What makes or breaks a UFO analysis in Chile often comes down to **evidence quality**:

* **Highquality evidence** radar data, highresolution video with metadata, synchronized multiobserver recordings gives investigators a strong basis for explanation or for ruling out known causes.
* **Lowerquality evidence**, such as blurry mobile phone videos or untimed eyewitness accounts, routinely leads to ambiguous or unresolved classifications because the uncertainty is too great to conclusively match to any known source.

This pattern reflects not just Chiles procedures but broader norms in UFO investigation: when the evidence supports a prosaic cause, experts generally opt for that explanation; when the evidence cannot be reconciled with known phenomena, the case is left as unidentified pending additional data. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidentified_flying_object" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentified flying object</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified flying objectAn unidentified flying object (UFO) is an object or phenomenon seen in the sky but not yet identified or exp...</span></span></span>

## Illustration: A Case That Remains Unresolved

The November 2014 Chilean Navy incident exemplifies how Chiles system handles hardtoexplain reports without defaulting to extraordinary claims:

* The sighting was recorded on infrared by trained military personnel.
* Radar and air traffic data were checked and showed no matching conventional aircraft.
* Analyses by multiple experts found no definitive prosaic explanation.

## Conclusion

Chilean UFO reports are analysed through a structured process that prioritises **objective evidence, expert review, and careful categorisation**. Most cases, like elsewhere in the world, turn out to be misidentifications of ordinary phenomena once adequate data is assembled. A minority often those involving professional observers and multiple corroborative data streams remain officially **unresolved as UAPs** but are handled with clear analytical boundaries that avoid unwarranted conclusions.
