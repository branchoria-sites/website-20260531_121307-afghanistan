## Introduction

In 2025, the United States military’s official reporting infrastructure recorded a small set of *Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena* (UAP) events involving units associated with **[Djibouti]({{ 'djibouti/' | relative_url }})** — a strategic U.S. base of operations in the Horn of Africa — as part of routine surveillance and anomaly‑resolution procedures. These mission reports, drawn from U.S. Department of War (DoW) and United States Africa Command (USAFRICOM) disclosures made public in 2026, *do not represent confirmed extraterrestrial encounters* but rather structured military filings of aerial phenomena that could not be immediately identified with existing air‑ and space‑surveillance assets. The key documents include a standardized mission report (MISREP) and a brief infrared (IR) sensor video clip, both of which are heavily redacted and provide limited context about the events under review.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://uapfilewatch.com/document/dow-uap-d8-mission-report-djibouti-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapfilewatch.com">[UAP File Watch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapfilewatch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dow uap d8 mission report djibouti 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP File WatchMission report detailing UAP observation in Mediterranean Sea, July 2025. · UAP Disclosure HubJuly 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## Origins and Redactions of 2025 Mission Reports

* **MISREP “DOW‑UAP‑D8, Mission Report, Djibouti, 2025”** – A formal military mission report filed under U.S. procedures for recording UAP encounters. The report’s metadata and public extract describe an observation of two “white hot” UAPs moving at approximately 240 nautical miles per hour. ## Observed UAP Characteristics in 2025 Reports

### Infrared and Thermal Signatures

The MISREP identifies the observed UAPs as “*white hot*,” a term consistent with **infrared (IR) sensor output** indicating that the contacts registered at the highest temperature range in the sensor palette. This suggests the use of a thermal imaging system during the encounter, a common tool in U.S. military aerial surveillance, but *does not by itself signify any extraordinary physical properties* beyond an intense IR signature relative to background.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/dow-uap-d8-mission-report-djibouti-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyTwo Round White-Hot UAPs at 240 Knots, Department of War · UFO TransparencyMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

### Speed and Motion Data

In the DOW‑UAP‑D8 report, the UAPs were described moving at approximately **240 knots (around 276 mph / 444 km/h)** in a southerly direction. Such a velocity falls within the *subsonic range* of many conventional aircraft and airborne objects. While considered noteworthy within the structured reporting system, this speed does *not inherently require non‑terrestrial propulsion* or behaviour beyond the limits of known aviation categories.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/dow-uap-d8-mission-report-djibouti-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyTwo Round White-Hot UAPs at 240 Knots, Department of War · UFO TransparencyMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

The USAFRICOM IR video does not provide detailed object classification. It depicts a *small region of contrast* traversing the frame over a two‑second span, but the limited duration and lack of additional sensor corroboration hinder conclusive interpretation. ## Interpretative Constraints and Assessment

### Redaction and Context Gaps

The most significant limitation in analysing these reports is **the heavy redaction of critical metadata** — for example, unit identifiers, mission context, sensor types, and precise timing. Without this context, assessing whether the reported UAP events differ materially from conventional airborne objects (such as aircraft, drones, atmospheric phenomena, or sensor artefacts) remains difficult.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/dow-uap-d8-mission-report-djibouti-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyTwo Round White-Hot UAPs at 240 Knots, Department of War · UFO TransparencyMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

### Absence of Corroborating Data

Neither of the disclosed documents offers *triangulated sensor data*, such as radar tracks, multispectral imaging, or multiple independent witnesses with synchronized recordings. In formal analysed UAP cases, such corroboration is typically essential for excluding known classes of aerial traffic or sensor phenomena. The absence of comprehensive corroborative evidence in these two Djibouti‑linked records means they remain categorised by analysts as **“unresolved”** rather than confirmed as anomalous in a scientific sense.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pursueufotracker.com/files/unresolved-uap-report-djibouti-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pursueufotracker.com">[Pursue UFO Tracker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pursueufotracker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">unresolved uap report djibouti 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pursue UFO TrackerUnresolved UAP Report, Djibouti, 2025 &#124; PURSUE UFO Tracker...</span></span></span>

### Official U.S. Military Position

U.S. Department of Defense and allied anomaly resolution offices (such as AARO) maintain that *lack of identification is not evidence of alien technology or non‑Earth origin*. These bodies treat such reports as part of ongoing data collection intended to reduce ambiguity in national security airspace monitoring; many unresolved cases reflect *sensor limitations, redaction policies, and analytical constraints*, not definitive anomalous technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2024</span></span></span>

Within the broader context of UAP documentation tied to Djibouti, the 2025 military mission reports are the most substantial *officially released data points* to date. They contribute to structured military record‑keeping but do *not establish recurring anomalous phenomena unique to Djibouti’s airspace* beyond what might be expected from routine military surveillance and reporting frameworks. These files are of interest for researchers tracking UAP reporting practices, sensor use, and data limitations but do not, on their face, resolve the nature or origin of the observed phenomena.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://uapfilewatch.com/document/dow-uap-d8-mission-report-djibouti-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapfilewatch.com">[UAP File Watch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapfilewatch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dow uap d8 mission report djibouti 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP File WatchMission report detailing UAP observation in Mediterranean Sea, July 2025. · UAP Disclosure HubJuly 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>
