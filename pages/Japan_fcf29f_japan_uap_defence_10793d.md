## Introduction

[Japan]({{ 'what-makes-japans-ufo-record-so-unusual/' | relative_url }})’s official approach to unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), often still called UFOs in public discussion, is fundamentally a defence and surveillance issue rather than an extraterrestrial one. Tokyo’s concern is not whether unexplained objects come from another world, but whether an unidentified object in or near Japanese airspace could represent a drone, balloon, intelligence platform, military aircraft, sensor anomaly, or another security risk. That distinction became especially important after 2020, when the Ministry of Defence introduced formal reporting procedures for unidentified objects and linked the subject directly to national defence responsibilities. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2020/09/14/national/japan-defense-ministry-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: japantimes.co.jp">[The Japan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">japantimes.co.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">japan defense ministry ufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Japan TimesJapanese Defense Ministry unveils protocol for...14 Sept 2020 — In a statement, Kono asked SDF members to record and phot...</span></span></span>

Japan’s position reflects its strategic environment. The country sits near heavily monitored sea lanes, hosts major American military facilities, and faces persistent concerns about foreign surveillance activities. In that setting, an unidentified object is treated first as an airspace-awareness problem. The result is a policy framework that emphasises reporting, documentation, intelligence analysis, and cooperation with the United States rather than public speculation about alien visitors. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://dkiapcss.edu/nexus_articles/a-comparative-survey-of-security-approaches-toward-unexplained-aerial-phenomena-across-the-indo-pacific/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dkiapcss.edu">[Asia-Pacific Center for Security Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dkiapcss.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Asia-Pacific Center for Security StudiesA Comparative Survey of Security Approaches Toward...by JE Reiss · 2023 · Cited by 3 — In 2020...</span></span></span>

## The 2020 Ministry of Defence instruction

The turning point came in September 2020 under Defence Minister Taro Kono. Following increased international attention to U.S. military UAP reporting, Kono issued standing instructions covering what the Japan Self-Defence Forces (JSDF) should do if personnel encountered unidentified objects that could affect national defence or security. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2020/09/14/national/japan-defense-ministry-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: japantimes.co.jp">[The Japan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">japantimes.co.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">japan defense ministry ufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Japan TimesJapanese Defense Ministry unveils protocol for...14 Sept 2020 — In a statement, Kono asked SDF members to record and phot...</span></span></span>

* Preserve information gathered during surveillance or airspace-monitoring operations.
* Conduct analysis of collected data.
* Consider information received through other channels, including civilian reports, when relevant to defence assessment. The practical significance of the order is often misunderstood. Japan did not create a dedicated UFO investigation office, announce recovered objects, or acknowledge extraterrestrial technology. Instead, the ministry formalised procedures for handling unusual observations that could affect military operations or airspace security. Kono himself emphasised that the issue was unidentified objects, not confirmed visitors from space. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2020/09/14/national/japan-defense-ministry-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: japantimes.co.jp">[The Japan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">japantimes.co.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">japan defense ministry ufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Japan TimesJapanese Defense Ministry unveils protocol for...14 Sept 2020 — In a statement, Kono asked SDF members to record and phot...</span></span></span>

In operational terms, the instruction fits naturally into Japan’s existing air-defence mission. The JSDF already tracks aircraft approaching Japanese airspace, responds to incursions, and maintains extensive radar and surveillance networks. The 2020 guidance effectively ensured that unusual observations would be captured within those existing systems rather than dismissed or handled inconsistently.

## Why unidentified objects became a security concern

Japan’s modern UAP discussion emerged during a broader shift in regional security thinking. The same years that saw increased UAP reporting also saw heightened concern about drones, intelligence gathering, missile technology, and surveillance platforms operating across East Asia.

For defence planners, an unidentified object does not need to be extraordinary to matter. A small drone, high-altitude balloon, electronic intelligence platform, or experimental aircraft can create significant security concerns even if it eventually receives an entirely conventional explanation.

This became especially clear after several international balloon incidents and growing awareness of unmanned systems. Defence officials increasingly viewed unexplained aerial observations through the lens of domain awareness: identifying what is present in the airspace, who controls it, and whether it poses a threat.

Japan’s geography amplifies that concern. The country monitors approaches across large maritime areas stretching from the East China Sea to the western Pacific. Any unidentified object appearing in those environments enters a surveillance network already focused on tracking military activity and maintaining situational awareness. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://dkiapcss.edu/nexus_articles/a-comparative-survey-of-security-approaches-toward-unexplained-aerial-phenomena-across-the-indo-pacific/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dkiapcss.edu">[Asia-Pacific Center for Security Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dkiapcss.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Asia-Pacific Center for Security StudiesA Comparative Survey of Security Approaches Toward...by JE Reiss · 2023 · Cited by 3 — In 2020...</span></span></span>

The result is that Japanese defence discussions rarely frame UAPs as a separate mystery category. Instead, they are folded into the broader challenge of distinguishing ordinary traffic, atmospheric effects, drones, balloons, satellites, sensor artefacts, and potential adversary systems.

## What Japan has and has not publicly confirmed

One reason Japan’s defence approach attracts confusion is the gap between public expectations and official statements.

### What has been confirmed

Japan has publicly confirmed that:

* The Ministry of Defence established reporting procedures for unidentified aerial objects in 2020.
* Self-Defence Forces personnel are expected to document and analyse unusual observations.
* The government monitors developments in U.S. UAP investigations with interest.
* Lawmakers and defence figures have argued that unidentified objects deserve attention because of possible national-security implications. ## U.S. cooperation and the influence of American reporting

Japan’s modern UAP policy cannot be separated from developments in the United States.

The Pentagon’s release of UAP-related material and the creation of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) helped normalise the idea that unidentified observations could be discussed without immediately implying extraterrestrial explanations. The American shift created a language of risk management, sensor analysis, and intelligence assessment that Japanese officials could adopt. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYJZp7lovKK/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: instagram.com">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">instagram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Trump administration has released classified UFO and ...The Trump administration has released classified UFO and UAP files for the fi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">rst time. In 2024, a cross-party group of Japanese lawmakers argued that the government should strengthen its capacity to gather and analyse information about unidentified phenomena and improve cooperation with the United States. Members explicitly linked the issue to the possibility that some observations could involve advanced foreign weapons or reconnaissance systems. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/japan-lawmakers-want-govt-guard-against-security-risks-ufos-2024-05-29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The move is prompted by findings from the U.S. government&#x27;s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) that reported numerous sightings...</span></span></span>

However, those maps do not demonstrate the presence of extraordinary craft.
