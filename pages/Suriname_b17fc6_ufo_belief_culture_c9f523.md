## Introduction

[Suriname]({{ 'what-surinames-ufo-stories-really-show/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history is not mainly a story about mysterious lights in the sky. It is also a story about how ideas travelled. The most distinctive contribution to this subject comes from researcher Tanya Wijngaarde, whose doctoral work argued that UFO belief in Suriname can be understood as the local reception of a largely American mythology rather than as an isolated set of unexplained sightings. Her research shifted attention away from the question of whether UFOs are physically real and towards a different question: how do narratives about aliens, secret governments, cosmic visitors, and hidden knowledge move between cultures? <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/42674404/Proefschrift.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uva.nl">[Pure]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uva.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pure Proefschrift</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PureProefschrift - UvA-DARE (Digital Academic Repository)11 Dec 2019 — Tanya Wijngaarde. Instead of treating the country as a source of major extraterrestrial encounters, Wijngaarde examined how global media, post-colonial cultural influence, religion, race, and popular entertainment shaped local belief. ## American UFO Ideas in a Surinamese Setting

Wijngaarde’s dissertation, *Ufo’s boven Suriname. De receptie van een Amerikaanse ideologie* (“UFOs Above Suriname: The Reception of an American Ideology”), explicitly framed UFO culture as an imported belief system. Rather than starting with aircraft reports, she focused on the cultural origins of UFO narratives and how those narratives were received in Suriname. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/42674404/Proefschrift.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uva.nl">[Pure]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uva.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pure Proefschrift</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PureProefschrift - UvA-DARE (Digital Academic Repository)11 Dec 2019 — Tanya Wijngaarde. Her argument begins with the observation that modern UFO mythology is deeply connected to the United States. The flying-saucer era emerged after the famous 1947 sightings and quickly became entangled with Cold War fears, science fiction, religious speculation, conspiracy theories, and ideas about technological superiority. Over decades, these themes became embedded in American popular culture through films, television programmes, magazines, and later internet communities. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindorama.com/ufos-boven-suriname-h-ramsoedh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindorama.com">[Hindorama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindorama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO&#x27;s boven Suriname</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HindoramaUFO&#x27;s boven Suriname - H. According to Wijngaarde, many of these themes appeared in Surinamese UFO discussions with surprisingly little alteration. Reports about benevolent extraterrestrials, hidden government knowledge, secret military projects, and alien contact echoed narratives already familiar from American UFO literature. What interested her was not simply that these stories existed in Suriname, but that they often carried assumptions that originated in a very different social environment. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://promotiekamer.nl/promovendi/tanya-wijngaarde-2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: promotiekamer.nl">[promotiekamer.nl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">promotiekamer.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Promovenda Tanya WijngaardeUit het onderzoek blijkt dat veel ufo-complottheorieën ook in Suriname worden verteld, inclusief de Amerikaans...</span></span></span>

The result was not straightforward copying. Surinamese believers interpreted UFO stories through local experiences, ethnic diversity, religious traditions, and post-colonial history. Yet the underlying structure of many accounts remained recognisably American. This led Wijngaarde to describe the phenomenon as a reception process: a foreign mythology entering a new cultural setting and being adapted rather than wholly reinvented. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://brill.com/display/book/9789004513877/BP000024.xml?language=en&amp;srsltid=AfmBOoqq17QaNrm8mbASiAKdWzw-mmiHECCK4_gGIIm3NKKVqBjCeTRS" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brill.com">[Brill]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brill.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tanya Wijngaarde. ## Why Race and Colonial History Became Part of the Discussion

One of the most unusual aspects of Wijngaarde’s research was her attention to race and colonial influence. Much UFO writing concentrates on sightings, government secrecy, or alleged extraterrestrial encounters. Her work instead examined the social ideas embedded within UFO narratives. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.folia.nl/wetenschap/134361/in-veel-ufo-verhalen-zijn-de-grote-blonde-aliens-moreel-superieur" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folia.nl">[Folia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folia.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in veel ufo verhalen zijn de grote blonde aliens moreel superieur</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folia&#x27;In veel ufo-verhalen zijn de grote, blonde aliens moreel...5 Dec 2019 — Buitenpromovendus Tanya Wijngaarde onderzocht hoe Amerikaa...</span></span></span>

In interviews discussing her dissertation, she pointed to recurring patterns in alien stories where physical appearance is linked to moral value. She noted that certain popular UFO traditions portray tall, blond extraterrestrials as enlightened or spiritually advanced, while other alien types are represented as cold, manipulative, or threatening. In her view, these narrative patterns reflected historical assumptions about hierarchy and identity that emerged within Western societies. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.folia.nl/wetenschap/134361/in-veel-ufo-verhalen-zijn-de-grote-blonde-aliens-moreel-superieur" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folia.nl">[Folia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folia.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in veel ufo verhalen zijn de grote blonde aliens moreel superieur</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folia&#x27;In veel ufo-verhalen zijn de grote, blonde aliens moreel...5 Dec 2019 — Buitenpromovendus Tanya Wijngaarde onderzocht hoe Amerikaa...</span></span></span>

Her research argued that some of these ideas travelled into Suriname together with the broader UFO mythology. Because Suriname experienced centuries of Dutch colonial rule and extensive exposure to European and American media, she suggested that imported cultural frameworks often carried authority. In that interpretation, UFO belief became another example of how global narratives can be adopted within a post-colonial society. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindorama.com/category/bibliotheek/page/43/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindorama.com">[Hindorama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindorama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HindoramaBIBLIOTHEEKTanya Wijngaarde Ufo&#x27;s boven Suriname. This aspect of the work attracted attention because it moved the discussion away from the usual believer-versus-sceptic debate. Instead of asking whether alien visitors existed, Wijngaarde asked what social messages UFO stories communicate and why those messages can remain influential when transferred across national and ethnic boundaries. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.folia.nl/wetenschap/134361/in-veel-ufo-verhalen-zijn-de-grote-blonde-aliens-moreel-superieur" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folia.nl">[Folia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folia.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in veel ufo verhalen zijn de grote blonde aliens moreel superieur</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folia&#x27;In veel ufo-verhalen zijn de grote, blonde aliens moreel...5 Dec 2019 — Buitenpromovendus Tanya Wijngaarde onderzocht hoe Amerikaa...</span></span></span>

## Local Believers, Lectures, and Small-Group Activity

The cultural-import argument was strengthened by the structure of Suriname’s UFO community itself. Available evidence suggests that the country never developed a large mass movement centred on UFOs. Instead, a relatively small network of enthusiasts created discussion spaces, reporting systems, lectures, and public events. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindorama.com/category/nieuws/nieuws-uit-suriname/page/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindorama.com">[Hindorama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindorama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nieuws uit SurinameTussen 2009 en 2015 werden ruim tachtig ufo&#x27;s gemeld bij het Surinaamse ufo-meldpunt. Following a widely publicised 2009 sighting that reached newspaper front pages, Wijngaarde and two collaborators established a Surinamese UFO reporting centre. Its purpose was not only to collect reports but also to examine possible explanations and document local experiences. Between 2009 and 2015, more than eighty reports were reportedly submitted to the organisation. Annual lectures and occasional film screenings attracted audiences, though the active core remained much smaller than the broader public attendance figures. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindorama.com/category/nieuws/page/18/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindorama.com">[Hindorama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindorama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIEUWSWijngaarde is al jaren geïnteresseerd in ufo&#x27;s en de verhalen erover. ## Why Culture Matters Alongside Sightings
