## Introduction

Malta’s UFO reports often reach the same point: witnesses publish photographs or videos, journalists ask whether any aircraft were in the area, and Malta Air Traffic Services (MATS) states that normal air traffic was accounted for. That answer can eliminate one category of explanation, but it rarely resolves the case itself. The result is a persistent verification gap. Reported objects may not match logged commercial flights, yet there is usually no publicly available investigative file, radar release, technical analysis, or official follow-up capable of determining what was actually recorded. In Malta, the question is therefore not only whether unusual aerial events occur, but why so few records exist that would allow them to be independently checked.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/It-s-not-a-bird-it-s-not-a-plane-it-may-be-a-UFO.428455" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It s not a bird it s not a plane it may be a UFO.428455</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Amateur astronomer David Pace said that the UFO in the picture looks very similar to the...Read more...</span></span></span>

Hundreds of aircraft transit the region daily, making aviation records potentially useful for identifying ordinary [explanations]({{ 'explanations-88b806/' | relative_url }}). Yet the country has never developed a public UFO or UAP archive comparable to those released in the United Kingdom, France, or the United States. As a result, many Maltese sightings remain suspended between explanation and uncertainty rather than being conclusively solved or confirmed.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.guidememalta.com/en/summer-surge-malta-air-traffic-services-hits-record-high-in-overflights-this-summer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guidememalta.com">[GuideMeMalta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guidememalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guide Me Malta Summer surge!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Malta Air Traffic Services hits record high in...30 Aug 2024 — The busiest day of air traffic ever recorded was Sunday 23rd June this ye...</span></span></span>

## What Malta Air Traffic Services Has Confirmed

The clearest example of official comment came during the wave of reported sightings and photographs that circulated in 2012. After images of an unusual object over Qawra and Birkirkara attracted attention, Malta Air Traffic Services was asked whether anything abnormal had appeared in monitored airspace.

MATS responded that, from an air-traffic-management perspective, all aircraft operating at the relevant time were accounted for and that no abnormal sightings had been reported through aviation channels. The statement is important because it establishes one verified fact: controllers did not identify an untracked conventional aircraft requiring investigation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/It-s-not-a-bird-it-s-not-a-plane-it-may-be-a-UFO.428455" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It s not a bird it s not a plane it may be a UFO.428455</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Amateur astronomer David Pace said that the UFO in the picture looks very similar to the...Read more...</span></span></span>

That type of confirmation has analytical value because it narrows the field of possibilities. If a photographed object does not correspond to a scheduled airliner, a filed flight plan, or another known aircraft under air-traffic control, several routine explanations become less likely. At the same time, the statement does not automatically validate an extraordinary interpretation. It only confirms that standard monitored traffic did not provide an obvious answer.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/It-s-not-a-bird-it-s-not-a-plane-it-may-be-a-UFO.428455" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It s not a bird it s not a plane it may be a UFO.428455</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Amateur astronomer David Pace said that the UFO in the picture looks very similar to the...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why Logged Aircraft Do Not Explain Every Image

Readers sometimes assume that a positive air-traffic check should immediately solve a sighting. In practice, aviation records answer only a limited set of questions.

Air-traffic controllers can generally identify aircraft operating under recognised procedures, but photographs and videos may capture:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Optical effects caused by camera sensors or compression artefacts.
* Weather balloons, drones, kites, birds, or atmospheric phenomena.
* Objects outside the observer's estimated distance and scale.
* Brief reflections, glare events, or lens effects.
* Misidentified military or non-standard aerial activity not obvious from a public image alone.

</div>

A photograph showing a disc-like or triangular shape therefore does not become more mysterious simply because scheduled aircraft were accounted for. The image may still lack enough information regarding altitude, speed, direction, size, duration, or environmental conditions to support any firm conclusion. This was effectively the position adopted by commentators examining Malta's 2012 reports. Amateur astronomer David Pace noted that one alleged flying-saucer image appeared striking but could not be confidently identified from the available evidence.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/It-s-not-a-bird-it-s-not-a-plane-it-may-be-a-UFO.428455" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It s not a bird it s not a plane it may be a UFO.428455</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Amateur astronomer David Pace said that the UFO in the picture looks very similar to the...Read more...</span></span></span>

The Marsaxlokk photograph published in 2013 illustrates the same limitation. The image was widely circulated and reportedly originated from a police officer who wished to remain anonymous. Yet no publicly released radar data, metadata analysis, witness interview record, or technical assessment accompanied the photograph. ## The Missing Public Archive Problem

The larger issue is structural rather than technological. Malta has no known public repository of UFO or UAP reports, no declassified national investigation programme, and no routinely published case files comparable to archives released elsewhere.

The contrast becomes clearer when compared with the United Kingdom. British authorities accumulated decades of UFO correspondence, sighting reports, policy discussions, and internal memoranda before eventually transferring large collections to The National Archives. Those files allow researchers to examine how reports were handled, what evidence was reviewed, and why officials reached particular conclusions.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reportsEarly letters regarding UFO sightings · Correspondence on the Rendlesham Forest incident · Documents on U...</span></span></span>

No equivalent Maltese archive is publicly available. As a result:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Many reported sightings survive only through newspaper coverage, social-media posts, or UFO websites.
* Original witness statements are often unavailable.
* Radar records are rarely released publicly.
* Follow-up investigations, if any occurred, are usually undocumented.
* Cases cannot easily be re-examined years later using primary evidence.

</div>

This creates a significant verification gap. Researchers attempting to assess a Maltese sighting often encounter secondary descriptions rather than original records. Even when witnesses appear sincere, the absence of supporting documentation makes independent evaluation difficult.

## Why Radar and Flight Data Rarely Reach the Public

Modern air-traffic systems generate large quantities of operational information, but that does not mean the data automatically enter the public domain.

Air-traffic records exist primarily for safety, navigation, and regulatory purposes. Releasing raw operational data can raise issues involving privacy, security, commercial aviation activity, and technical interpretation. Consequently, even when controllers have relevant information, the public may receive only a summary statement rather than detailed records.

Malta's position at the centre of busy Mediterranean routes adds another complication. Airspace management involves interactions between domestic authorities, international aviation procedures, and neighbouring flight-information regions. A sighting occurring near Malta may involve information held across multiple systems rather than a single easily accessible national file.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://maltats.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MATS-AR-2019.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maltats.com">[Mats &#124; Mats]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maltats.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mats &#124; MatsMALTA AIR TRAFFIC SERVICES LIMITEDWe are accountable to the Maltese public, users of our airspace and all our stakeholders. For UFO researchers, this means that the existence of records should not be confused with public availability. Data may exist without being released in a form that allows independent scrutiny.

## Thin Records Encourage Competing Narratives

When official documentation is scarce, interpretation tends to fragment.

Supporters of anomalous explanations often point to the absence of an identified aircraft as evidence that something unusual occurred. Skeptics counter that poor-quality imagery and missing contextual information prevent any extraordinary conclusion. Both sides can draw attention to the same evidentiary gap because neither possesses enough information to settle the matter decisively.

This pattern appears repeatedly across Maltese reports.
