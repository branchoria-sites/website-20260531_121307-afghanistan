## Introduction

In late 2024, a well‑documented case on the Côte d’Azur highlighted how ground‑based event illumination from [Monaco]({{ 'are-monaco-ufo-sightings-really/' | relative_url }}) can be misinterpreted as unexplained aerial phenomena across the border. ## The 2024 Villefranche‑sur‑Mer Report

At **19:01 local time on 25 November 2024**, a witness in Villefranche‑sur‑Mer, roughly 9.5 km west of Monaco, observed **five to ten white circular luminous structures moving in a circular pattern** against a backdrop of low, stratocumulus cloud. The witness captured video of the event, enabling a detailed analysis. GEIPAN’s investigation concluded that the observed phenomenon **very probably stemmed from several ground‑based powerful projectors (skytrackers) directed upward**, whose beams reflected and scattered off the base of the cloud deck, creating the appearance of moving luminous circles. GEIPAN classified this as a **Class B** case — an event with sufficient data to identify a probable explanation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60950?page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VILLEFRANCHE-SUR-MER (06) 25.11.2024 | GEIPANFebruary 13, 2025 — VILLEFRANCHE-SUR-MER (06) 25.11.2024 Observation date 25/11/2024 region...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2025</span></span></span>

The report notes that at the **exact time and direction** of the sighting, the show entitled *“Stand Up Monaco”* was beginning at Monaco’s **Grimaldi Forum**. ## How Skytrackers Appear from a Distance

Skytrackers are large, high‑intensity searchlights or event projectors used in promotional displays, concerts, festivals and large‑scale public events. When aimed upward at night, especially toward **low cloud layers**, these beams can illuminate cloud bases, producing **diffuse bright areas, circular halos or apparent motion** as the beam intersects with moving cloud structure. From a distance and without clear reference points, observers can misinterpret these as moving luminous objects high in the sky. While not common in everyday urban lighting, their effect can be striking when visible across water or from neighbouring towns. This mechanism is consistent with various documented cases worldwide in which strong ground lighting interacting with atmospheric conditions was initially mistaken for something anomalous. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60950?page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VILLEFRANCHE-SUR-MER (06) 25.11.2024 | GEIPANFebruary 13, 2025 — VILLEFRANCHE-SUR-MER (06) 25.11.2024 Observation date 25/11/2024 region...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why Coastal Sightlines Cross Borders

The geography of the **Riviera coast** — with narrow coastal plains meeting steep hills and the Mediterranean Sea — allows distant light sources to propagate over open water and be visible from dozens of kilometres away. In the Villefranche‑sur‑Mer case, the sighting direction (east‑northeast) and the position of the cloud layer made it possible for the Monaco event lights to be seen well beyond the Monegasque border. Even modest elevation changes along the coast permit unobstructed sightlines that amplify the visual reach of powerful beams. This means that events within Monaco — public performances, festivals, promotional lighting — can create luminous displays that are visible and potentially puzzling from adjacent French towns. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60950?page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VILLEFRANCHE-SUR-MER (06) 25.11.2024 | GEIPANFebruary 13, 2025 — VILLEFRANCHE-SUR-MER (06) 25.11.2024 Observation date 25/11/2024 region...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2025</span></span></span>

## Distinguishing Artificial Beams from Aerial Objects

When civilians report unexplained lights, distinguishing between genuine aerial objects and **artificial beams interacting with atmospheric conditions** is crucial. In the Villefranche‑sur‑Mer case, the presence of stratocumulus cloud at approximately 2000 m, the duration of the display (about five minutes), and the alignment with an organised event strongly support an artificial light explanation. ## Takeaway: Event Lights vs Unidentified Phenomena

The Monaco‑related skytracker sightings illustrate that **powerful event lighting from Monaco can project visible structures into the night sky** that may be misreported as unidentified aerial phenomena.
