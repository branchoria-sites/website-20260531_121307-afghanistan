## Introduction

This chronology organises the known reports of unidentified flying object (UFO) sightings and related encounters within **[Guyana]({{ 'what-we-know-about-ufo-sightings-in/' | relative_url }})’s national context**, drawing primarily on publicly accessible databases such as the **National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC)** and available news coverage. Over several decades, witnesses from interior river regions to urban Georgetown have described luminous objects, unusual aerial manoeuvres, and other anomalous phenomena. The record is **sparse and uneven**, with incidents typically remaining unverified and lacking official investigation data, but nonetheless offers a diachronic view of how such claims have emerged in different settings across the country.[thephenomenon.app]

## 1971–1987: Frontier and Isolated Reports

**15 August 1971 – New River area:** One of the oldest known entries in the NUFORC database describes four observers on a surveying trip near the New River / Courentyne River area reporting a *“large silver saucer”* hovering and allegedly drawing up river water with an inverted funnel structure. The event is said to have lasted ten minutes before the object ascended and disappeared. This account was only submitted decades later and does **not** appear to have contemporaneous corroboration from press or [official records]({{ 'official-records-b5d75e/' | relative_url }}).[thephenomenon.app]

**10 August 1975 – Unspecified Guyana:** A witness recounted observing a fast-moving light that appeared to circle near stars within the night sky for some minutes. Such descriptions in the dataset often lack details that would allow firm identification against known celestial bodies or aircraft.[thephenomenon.app]

**15 December 1987 – Guyana-wide (approximate):** Another early sighting involves a reddish-orange object streaking rapidly into the sky over about five seconds. The brevity and colour characteristics suggest it could align with atmospheric re-entries or meteoric phenomena, though the report itself is classified simply as “unknown” without explanatory resolution.[thephenomenon.app]

## 2000s: Urban and Multiple-Witness Sightings

**24 February 2006 – Guyana (general):** A group of seven people reported seeing a **cigar-shaped, brightly lit object** flying silently at low altitude, with witnesses noting what appeared to be flames trailing behind it. Although intriguing in its group witness aspect, there is no formal follow-up beyond the NUFORC entry, and such descriptions are reminiscent of misidentifications of aircraft or atmospheric effects.[thephenomenon.app]

**24 February 2007 – Georgetown:** Over Georgetown, several witnesses observed an object initially resembling an aircraft due to blinking lights. The witness account emphasises unusual manoeuvre: a sudden stop followed by motion in an unexpected direction. The lack of shape detail and absence of corroborating radar or photography limits definitive interpretation.[thephenomenon.app]

## 2010s: Photographic Claims and Regional Reports

**29 August 2012 – Georgetown:** A sighting of a *“bright bluish flying saucer”* was claimed based on photographic analysis. The witness only noticed the object after reviewing the image on a computer, a common pattern in database reports where visual evidence appears post-factum. The report notes movement and changing shape between exposures.[thephenomenon.app]

**21 February 2013 – Crabwood Creek:** An entry describes a spherical object seen for about 30 seconds with alleged electrical or magnetic effects, including the witness’s phone shutting down during recording. This report is unusual in that the witness chose to remain anonymous and did not provide contact information or the purported video, complicating any external verification.[thephenomenon.app]

## 2016: Reports and Local Media Accounts

While the NUFORC index for 2016 does not list additional distinct Guyana entries beyond earlier years, **local news reports from November 2016** document heightened public interest in unidentified aerial phenomena. Residents near **Port Kaituma** noted an *unidentified aircraft circling* in airspace above the local airstrip and along river routes, prompting at least preliminary investigative interest by the **Guyana Defence Force (GDF)** and police due to uncertainty about the craft’s identity. ## Patterns and Credibility Issues

Across decades of reports, several patterns emerge:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Sparse Documentation:** Most sightings are singular witness accounts submitted years after the purported event, which complicates efforts to verify details or test alternative explanations.
* **Lack of Official Records:** There is little evidence of officially archived governmental analyses of these events in Guyana, with data primarily residing in independent witness databases like NUFORC.
* **Typical Phenomenology:** Descriptions of fast lights, sudden movements, and unexpected direction changes are common in civilian UFO reports globally, making attribution to misidentified conventional phenomena (aircraft, satellites, meteors) a plausible hypothesis absent further data.[NUFORC]

## Concluding Timeline Overview

The chronology of reported UFO sightings in Guyana remains limited in number and depth but reflects a longitudinal pattern of civilian reports spanning inland rivers, rural sky events, and urban environments. From the interior New River report in 1971 to the Georgetown photographic account of 2012 and the Port Kaituma curiosity in 2016, the evidence base consists predominantly of witness narratives rather than corroborated sensor or investigative data. Each case contributes to the broader portrait of how aerial anomalies are perceived and recorded within the country, but none stands as independently confirmed beyond witness testimony within the available public record.[thephenomenon.app]
