## Introduction

In Malawi, there is **no dedicated government archive or official mechanism** specifically for reporting, documenting, and studying unidentified flying objects (UFOs) or unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP). Unlike nations such as the United States or the United Kingdom, where historical government files on UFO/UAP observations have been retained and in some cases published by national archives, **Malawi has no publicly known equivalent programme or centralised repository** for such reports. Instead, any sightings or unusual aerial events are handled through existing aviation, police, and public record structures, with no specialised UFO archive or systematic public catalogue available.

## Civil Aviation Authority Protocols and Records

The **Malawi Civil Aviation Authority (MCAA)** is the statutory body responsible for oversight of civil aviation safety and airspace management in Malawi under the **Civil Aviation Act (2017)**. It maintains records related to civil aircraft registration, licences, and aviation operations and is charged with safety, navigation, and air traffic rules consistent with international standards. However, the Act’s record‑keeping provisions refer to standard aviation documentation — aircraft registrations, titles, licences and other regulatory data — and do not extend to UFO or anomalous aerial reports as a specific category.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://history.state.gov//countries/archives/malawi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[malawi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives - Countries - Office of the HistorianWORLD WIDE DIPLOMATIC ARCHIVES INDEX: MALAWI LEGISLATION The National Archives Act of 1990...</span></span></span>

While the MCAA oversees airspace safety and compliance for all civil aircraft, **there is no publicly accessible indication that it has a formal process for collecting or archiving reports of unexplained aerial phenomena** in a centralised or researchable institutional archive.

The Authority’s mandate focuses on technical aviation standards and safety rather than unexplained aerial occurrences.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://history.state.gov//countries/archives/malawi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[malawi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives - Countries - Office of the HistorianWORLD WIDE DIPLOMATIC ARCHIVES INDEX: MALAWI LEGISLATION The National Archives Act of 1990...</span></span></span> Civil Aviation Authority - MCAA

## National Archives and Government Records

Malawi does have a formal National Archives institution — the **National Archives of Malawi** — which serves as the repository for public records, historical documents, and government archives under statutes such as the National Archives Act and Printed Publications Act. Its mission is to preserve documentary heritage, regulate public records, and facilitate research access.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_of_Malawi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives of Malawi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives of Malawi</span></span></span>

However, there is **no public evidence or reference within accessible Malawi government records that these archives maintain a specialised collection of UFO or anomalous aerial phenomena reports**. The National Archives’ mandate covers a broad range of historical, judicial, and general state records, but its publicly described collection priorities do not include unexplained aerial phenomena or UFO data as a distinct archival category.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_of_Malawi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives of Malawi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives of Malawi</span></span></span>

For example, the **U.S. National Archives and Records Administration** maintains a curated collection of UFO/UAP‑related documents from federal agencies, and the UK National Archives holds Ministry of Defence records on public UFO reports from historical investigations.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Police Reports and Law Enforcement Handling

When unusual aerial events occur in Malawi — for instance, dramatic sightings or falling objects seen by local communities — the **police are often the first official responders**, typically assessing safety and requesting technical analysis from relevant agencies where possible. There is no indication that Malawi’s police have established a **centralised, searchable archive dedicated to UFO reports or unexplained aerial phenomena**, leaving any such incident records within routine law enforcement case files rather than a specialised UFO archive.

In cases reported in the media, police statements have stressed caution and scientific investigation of falling objects or unusual sightings, rather than classifying them as UFO phenomena warranting dedicated archival or investigative units. Media accounts from Karonga District in 2025 and earlier “UFO” stories show **police involvement focusing on public safety and identification of mundane explanations**, with no official repository for the incidents beyond routine documentation.

## Comparison with International Archives

Globally, some countries have developed formal or declassified archives related to aerial phenomena. In the United States, the national archival system organises UFO/UAP records by record groups and has increasingly incorporated such material in ongoing collections open for research.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

The United Kingdom’s Ministry of Defence historically maintained UFO investigation files that were later transferred to the **UK National Archives**; these include public correspondence and sighting reports retained over decades.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_of_Malawi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives of Malawi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives of Malawi</span></span></span>

In contrast, **Malawi lacks an equivalent dedicated archive**. No official policy documents, aviation safety narratives, or national archive listings show that the government or any civil authority systematically collects, classifies, or makes public unexplained aerial sighting reports as a distinct dataset. Instead, any documentation remains dispersed across general administrative, police, and aviation records if kept at all.

## Practical Implications of the Absence of a UFO Archive

The absence of a formal UFO or anomalous phenomena archive in Malawi has several practical consequences:

* **Inconsistent Documentation:** Without a dedicated reporting or record‑keeping mechanism, sightings are documented inconsistently, if at all, and may be lost within unrelated police or administrative files.
* **Limited Research Capacity:** Researchers and the public cannot readily access a central repository of reports for study or verification.
* **Reliance on External or Civilian Archives:** Interested parties must turn to independent databases such as the **National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC)** or global aggregators, which collect civilian reports but have no official governmental authority.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Latest UFO SightingsSeptember 6, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2021</span></span></span>
* **Public Communication Constraints:** In the absence of clear reporting channels, local authorities often emphasise conventional explanations for unusual sky events, framing them within safety and scientific investigation rather than UFO phenomena per se.

Overall, Malawi’s governance and archival systems do not currently include a specialised structure for [UFO sightings]({{ 'ufo-sightings/' | relative_url }}) or unidentified aerial events, leaving such material uncentralised and reliant on general record‑keeping frameworks without a designated UFO archive.
