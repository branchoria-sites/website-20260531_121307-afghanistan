## Introduction

In late December 2024, a flight‑crew UFO sighting over **[The Bahamas]({{ 'the-bahamas/' | relative_url }})** attracted significant media attention and revived public interest in unidentified aerial phenomena in this region. A private jet crew on a Surjet flight reported seeing one or more glowing, fast‑moving objects at very high altitude — an encounter that lasted around 45 minutes and was captured on video by a flight attendant. ## What the Crew Reported Over The Bahamas

On the **night of 23 December 2024**, a private jet operated by Surjet was returning to **Fort Lauderdale** from a flight over The Bahamas. The aircraft, with two pilots and a flight attendant aboard but no passengers, was cruising at approximately 43 000–45 000 feet when the crew received a radio message from **Miami air traffic control** about an unidentified object near their flight path. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/ufo-sighting-fort-lauderdale-flight-crew-spots-something-strange-in-the-sky/3511543/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nbcmiami.com">[NBC 6 South Florida]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nbcmiami.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NBC 6 South Florida UFO sighting?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Lauderdale flight crew spots something...January 10, 2025 — 9 Jan 2025 — A flight crew was flying back to Fort Lauderdale in an emp...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

## Limits of the Evidence and Missing Data

Despite the flight crew’s visible footage and testimony, **no official investigation report** (from civil aviation authorities, Bahamian government agencies, or international aviation safety bodies) has been published that confirms the object’s identity or airborne characteristics. There is also no known release of flight recorder data, radar logs from regional air traffic control, or corroborating sensor evidence made public at this time. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/ufo-sighting-fort-lauderdale-flight-crew-spots-something-strange-in-the-sky/3511543/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nbcmiami.com">[NBC 6 South Florida]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nbcmiami.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NBC 6 South Florida UFO sighting?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Lauderdale flight crew spots something...January 10, 2025 — 9 Jan 2025 — A flight crew was flying back to Fort Lauderdale in an emp...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

Key missing data for a rigorous resolution include:

* **Independent radar or ADS‑B tracking logs** showing an echo corresponding to the object reported by Miami ATC.
* **Multisensor confirmation** (e.g., infrared, secondary radar, satellite) that might help rule out atmospheric or astronomical phenomena.

* **Official reports or notices** from civil aviation authorities regarding potential flight safety hazards.

## Why High‑Altitude Aircraft Sightings Are Hard to Resolve

Encounters reported by flight crews attract attention because the witnesses are trained observers in a controlled environment. Yet such sightings over The Bahamas and elsewhere often remain unresolved due to known factors that complicate aerial perception:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Visual illusions at high altitude:** At 40 000 feet and above, depth perception and relative motion cues can be misleading without reference points.
* **Atmospheric effects:** Light scatter, auroral activity, or transient ionospheric conditions can alter perceived colour and motion.
* **Lack of corroborating sensors:** Without data from secondary radar, transponders, or wider air‑traffic monitoring systems, visual accounts cannot reliably exclude terrestrial or celestial causes.
* **Astronomical objects:** Bright planets, satellites, re‑entered rocket bodies, or meteors can appear stationary or move unpredictably relative to an aircraft’s motion.

</div>

In many historical cases of pilot or crew sightings, initial descriptions that seem unusual often align with prosaic explanations once additional sensor data or environmental analysis is available. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidentified_flying_object" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentified flying object</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified flying object</span></span></span>

## How This Sighting Fits The Bahamas UFO Record

Within the broader catalogue of Bahamian UFO reports — which include land‑based sightings, maritime observations, and cruise‑ship reports logged in citizen databases — the **Surjet flight crew incident** stands out because of its duration and the involvement of aviation professionals. However, like other reports from the region, it remains **unresolved** in terms of identifying the object’s physical nature, provenance, or mechanism. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thephenomenon.app/bahamas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thephenomenon.app">[thephenomenon.app]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thephenomenon.app</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Phenomenon | The Bahamas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phenomenon | The Bahamas</span></span></span>

Given the lack of cross‑verified data, the encounter does **not** constitute evidence of anomalous technology or extraterrestrial craft, but it does underscore how high‑altitude sightings — especially over busy air corridors near The Bahamas — can challenge simple classification.
