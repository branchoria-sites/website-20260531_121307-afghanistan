## Introduction

In [Vanuatu]({{ 'what-really-explains-vanuatus-strange/' | relative_url }}), one of the first questions to ask about a reported UFO light is not “What was flying?” but “Which volcano was active?” Across the archipelago, volcanic systems regularly generate red glows, flashing bursts, illuminated steam clouds, ash plumes and horizon reflections that can appear startlingly artificial at night. In some locations, these effects are so common that they form the country's strongest and most practical filter against misidentifying ordinary natural phenomena as unexplained aerial objects. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vmgd.gov.vu/geohazards/volcanoes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vmgd.gov.vu">[vmgd.gov.vu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vmgd.gov.vu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VMGDThe volcanic alert Level for East Epi submarine has been at the Level 1 since 1st February 2023. ## Why volcanic light fools witnesses

Volcanoes do not merely produce lava. They create entire lighting environments.

An active vent can illuminate ash, steam and low cloud from below, producing diffuse glowing shapes that seem detached from the ground. When observers are many kilometres away, terrain can hide the volcano itself while leaving only the glow visible. The result may resemble a floating object above the horizon rather than an eruption source. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vmgd.gov.vu/geohazards/volcanoes/benbow-and-marum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vmgd.gov.vu">[vmgd.gov.vu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vmgd.gov.vu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">akes that were visible at night. ## Tanna: the country's most obvious UFO-light trap

The clearest example is Mount Yasur on Tanna.

Yasur is among the world's most continuously active volcanoes. The Vanuatu Meteorology and Geo-Hazards Department describes it as a volcano characterised by ongoing Strombolian and Vulcanian activity, while historical [records]({{ 'records-gap-f983d7/' | relative_url }}) trace persistent eruptive behaviour back to at least the eighteenth century. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vmgd.gov.vu/geohazards/volcanoes/yasur" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vmgd.gov.vu">[vmgd.gov.vu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vmgd.gov.vu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">n for Yasur. Travel accounts frequently describe seeing Yasur's glow from considerable distances, and the volcano is famous enough that Captain Cook reportedly noticed its illumination from offshore during the eighteenth century. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://christoph.today/vanuatu-volcano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: christoph.today">[Christoph Today]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">christoph.today</span><span class="citation-popover-title">vanuatu volcano</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is one of the most active volcanoes in the world. The important analytical point is that a report of a red, flashing or hovering light near Tanna starts with a strong conventional explanation already available. Any unexplained-sighting claim from the region must first eliminate Yasur's activity using eruption records, weather conditions and viewing geometry.

## Ambrym and the lava-lake illusion

Ambrym provides an even more striking example because it has historically hosted lava lakes.

The volcano's Benbow and Marum vents became famous for molten lava lakes visible from the air and detectable from space. ## Other islands where volcanic activity changes the sky

## What remains after the volcanic filter?

The existence of volcanic misidentifications does not automatically explain every unusual sighting in Vanuatu. Aircraft, satellites, meteors, atmospheric optics and ordinary observational errors remain possible contributors. A small number of reports may still lack enough information for a definitive explanation.

However, the country's volcanic environment changes the burden of proof. In many parts of Vanuatu, especially around Tanna, Ambrym and other active systems, a mysterious night-time glow is not evidence of something extraordinary by default. It is evidence that volcanic activity must be ruled out first.

That makes volcano glow less a minor possibility than the central screening mechanism in Vanuatu's UFO landscape.
