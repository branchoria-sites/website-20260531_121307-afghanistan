## Introduction

Many reported “UFO” lights in [Vanuatu]({{ 'what-really-explains-vanuatus-strange/' | relative_url }}) are best understood by starting with a simpler question: what else was in the sky or on the sea at that moment? The country’s geography makes ordinary objects unusually difficult to judge. A bright satellite can appear to glide silently over a dark island. A distant aircraft can seem stationary above the ocean horizon. Fishing vessels, cargo ships and marine work lights can produce glows that look detached from the sea, especially when viewed across long stretches of water at night.

## Why Vanuatu's geography makes lights hard to identify

On a large continental landmass, people often judge distance using buildings, roads, mountains or city lights. Much of Vanuatu lacks those visual references at night.

A witness looking west from an island shoreline may be observing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A satellite hundreds of kilometres above Earth.
* An aircraft tens or hundreds of kilometres away.
* A ship beyond the visible horizon whose lights are being refracted by atmospheric conditions.
* Fishing vessels using powerful illumination systems.
* Reflections from clouds, haze or moisture over the sea.
* Volcanic glow or steam-related light effects near active volcanic regions.

</div>

Because the horizon is often dark and featureless, the brain can misjudge both distance and speed. A slow-moving satellite may appear much closer than it is. A distant aircraft approaching directly towards an observer may seem motionless for several minutes before suddenly changing apparent direction.

This problem is not unique to Vanuatu, but the archipelago's combination of ocean viewing angles and limited light pollution makes it particularly relevant when assessing unusual-light reports.

## Starlink trains and satellite passes

The most common modern source of “strange moving lights” reports worldwide is the Starlink satellite constellation operated by SpaceX. Shortly after launch, groups of Starlink satellites can appear as a line of bright lights moving together across the sky. Observers often describe them as a train, chain or convoy of objects. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.space.com/starlink-satellite-train-how-to-see-and-track-it" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: space.com">[Space]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">space.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Starlink satellite train: how to see and track it in the night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These satellites often create a &quot;Starlink satellite train&quot; — a captivating formation of closely grouped, bright lights moving in a line a...</span></span></span>

Several characteristics strongly favour a satellite explanation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The lights move in a straight line.

* No sound is heard.
* They do not visibly manoeuvre.

### Why satellites sometimes look stranger near the horizon

Observers often expect satellites to move steadily. In reality, brightness can fluctuate dramatically.

Research into Starlink visibility has shown that satellite brightness depends heavily on viewing angle and sunlight reflection geometry. Under some conditions, satellites can brighten suddenly, fade, then brighten again, creating the impression of controlled movement or flashing behaviour. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07805" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXivA Flat-Panel Brightness Model for the Starlink Satellites and Measurement of their Absolute Visual MagnitudeMarch 17, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2020</span></span></span>

Near the horizon, this effect becomes more dramatic because the observer is looking through a thicker layer of atmosphere. A witness may therefore report:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A light that appears to stop.
* A light that repeatedly brightens and dims.
* Multiple lights appearing in sequence.
* A cluster of lights that seem connected.

</div>

Such descriptions often appear unusual when reported from memory but can be consistent with known satellite behaviour.

## Aircraft lights over the islands and ocean

Commercial and regional aircraft remain one of the most overlooked explanations in Vanuatu sighting reports.

The country sits within Pacific air corridors linking Australia, New Zealand, Fiji, New Caledonia and other island states. Aircraft seen from the ground may appear unfamiliar because observers are viewing them across water rather than over land.

Several common visual effects create confusion.

### The "stationary aircraft" illusion

An aircraft flying towards an observer can appear nearly motionless because its forward movement produces little sideways motion across the viewer's field of vision.

For several minutes it may resemble:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* A hovering object.
* A bright star.
* A slowly pulsing light.

</div>

Only when the aircraft changes direction or passes overhead does its true nature become obvious.

### Landing lights can be visible for long distances

Modern aircraft landing lights are extremely bright. At night, they can be visible from remarkable distances, particularly across clear ocean air.

From an island coastline, a distant aircraft may therefore appear as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A single intense white light.
* A glowing orb.
* A light apparently suspended over the sea.

</div>

The absence of visible aircraft structure often encourages more exotic interpretations.

### Flashing patterns are useful clues

Aircraft navigation systems normally produce recognisable light behaviour:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* White strobes.
* Red anti-collision beacons.
* Green and red navigation lights on opposite wings.

</div>

If witnesses report regular rhythmic flashing, an aircraft explanation becomes significantly more likely than a satellite explanation.

## Ships, cargo traffic and fishing lights

Marine lights deserve special attention in Vanuatu because many witnesses naturally focus on the sky even when the original source may be at sea.

Ocean horizons remove depth cues. A vessel that is actually dozens of kilometres away may appear suspended above the waterline.

Powerful fishing lights have generated mystery-light reports in many parts of the Pacific and elsewhere. Fishing operations frequently use intense illumination to attract marine life, creating bright concentrations of light visible from long distances. ### Atmospheric refraction over water

Temperature differences between ocean layers and air masses can bend light.

This can produce:

* Apparent elevation above the horizon.
* Distorted shapes.
* Multiple images of a single light source.
* Lights that seem to float.

Such effects are especially relevant around island environments where observers frequently look across long marine distances at night.

## The Epi Island lights and competing explanations

## A practical checklist for witnesses

The most useful evidence often comes from information collected during the first few minutes of a sighting.

Before assuming a light is anomalous, record:

1. **Exact time and date.**
2. **Viewing direction** using a compass bearing if possible.
3. **Approximate elevation** above the horizon.
4. **Weather conditions**, including cloud cover and visibility.
5. **Movement pattern**: straight line, hovering, zig-zagging or drifting.
6. **Colour changes** and brightness changes.
7. **Sound**, including whether there was none.
8. **Photographs or video** with nearby landmarks visible.

Afterwards, check:

* Starlink and satellite tracking services.
