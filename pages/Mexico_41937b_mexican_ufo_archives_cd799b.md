## Introduction

Government records and public transparency systems in **Mexico** do contain documentation related to unidentified aerial phenomena — often labelled “UFOs” or **unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAPs)** — but these archives are best understood as repositories of **reports, agency correspondence and public requests for information**, rather than proof of extraterrestrial presence. The availability of such materials reflects modern transparency laws and archival practices in Mexico, which give researchers, journalists and citizens the legal right to request government records and consult historical files, even when the material is partial or inconclusive.

## DFS and National Archives: What the Official Files Actually Represent

One of the central institutional sources for UFO‑related material in the Mexican archive system is the documentation originally held by the **Dirección Federal de Seguridad (DFS)** — the Federal Security Directorate, a former intelligence and security service active from 1947 until its dissolution in 1985. Although the DFS’s core mission was espionage and internal security during the Cold War era, its extensive archives now reside under the custody of the **Archivo General de la Nación (AGN)**, Mexico’s national archive agency, which aims to preserve and provide access to the documentary memory of the nation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archivo_General_de_la_Naci%C3%B3n_%28M%C3%A9xico%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archivo General de la Nación (México</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archivo General de la Nación (México</span></span></span>

The DFS archive comprises millions of documents spanning decades of domestic intelligence activity, originally intended to monitor political movements and security threats. While UFO or anomalous aerial event references appear sporadically in such material — typically as reports sent by local authorities, security agents or civilians — they are part of a broader set of intelligence files, not a specialised extraterrestrial dossier. Scholarly and archival commentary emphasises that these files are most useful for reconstructing political history and intelligence practices in Mexico, rather than demonstrating evidence of alien phenomena. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://archivos.gob.mx/Legajos/pdf/Legajos15/06Elarchivo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivos.gob.mx">[Archivos]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivos.gob.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archivos El archivo de la Dirección Federal de Seguridad: una fuente</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seguridad (DFS) no es solo el archivo de la Guerra Sucia. ## Transparency Mechanisms: National Platform and Public Requests

The **Plataforma Nacional de Transparencia (National Transparency Platform)** is the principal mechanism for citizens to request and receive government records, including those involving UFO sightings or UAP reports. The platform centralises requests under Mexico’s general transparency regime, giving users online access to **both proactively published data and agency responses to specific queries**. It combines systems for filing requests, managing appeals and consulting obligatory disclosures from public institutions. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://archivos.gob.mx/Legajos/pdf/Legajos15/06Elarchivo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivos.gob.mx">[Archivos]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivos.gob.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archivos El archivo de la Dirección Federal de Seguridad: una fuente</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seguridad (DFS) no es solo el archivo de la Guerra Sucia. In practice, numerous requests related to UFO sightings, aerial phenomena or government involvement have been logged on the platform. These tend to yield **responses that reference existing reports, official statements or previously released files**, rather than unveiling new or sensational hidden material. Such transparency records illustrate public curiosity and the demand for accountability, but they also typically reflect the **limited scope of formal documentation** available about these phenomena within Mexican government archives. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://corrientealterna.unam.mx/cultura/ovnis-plataforma-nacional-de-transparencia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corrientealterna.unam.mx">[Corriente Alterna]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corrientealterna.unam.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Corriente AlternaOvnis y otros fenómenos en la Plataforma Nacional de TransparenciaJuly 3, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 3, 2022</span></span></span>

One recurring pattern in transparency responses is that agencies provide **copies of documents already released** through press releases or public reports — for example, on the 2004 Mexican Air Force infrared recordings of unidentified targets over Campeche — rather than previously classified material. This underscores the distinction between **media‑circulated incidents** and archival evidence accessible through legal requests.

### What Archives Do *Not* Reveal


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Verifiable evidence of extraterrestrial craft:** While surface references to unidentified sightings exist, archived material generally consists of **reports, correspondence and routine agency notes**, not confirmed physical evidence of non‑terrestrial origin.
* **Secret UFO programmes:** There is no substantiated indication in Mexican archives of clandestine programmes or classified files proving alien contact. Released and accessible documents are historical and administrative in nature, rather than evidentiary.
* **Complete raw datasets:** Much historical intelligence material remains organised primarily for governance or legal purposes, not for scientific analysis of UAPs, and may be incomplete or redacted when declassified.

## Differences Between Mexican and Foreign Archival Practice

For perspective, institutions such as the **U.S. National Archives** maintain separate categories and indexed collections related to unidentified aerial phenomena and make them publicly accessible online, often with detailed descriptions and catalog entries. These collections (e.g., in record groups dedicated to military or government investigations) prioritise cataloguing material linked to UFO/UAP reporting, reflecting a more explicit archival structure for these phenomena. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesRecords Related to Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs) and...February 15, 2024 — 24 Apr 2025 — NARA has records related...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 15, 2024</span></span></span>

In contrast, in Mexico the archival presence of UFO‑related documentation is **embedded** within broader historical intelligence files rather than segregated into a UFO‑specific collection. This means that researchers often need to piece together relevant material from general intelligence and public request archives, rather than consulting a dedicated UFO dossier.

## Public Engagement and Ongoing Debate

The combination of archival holdings and transparency mechanisms has given rise to **public debate and scholarly interest** around UFO reporting in Mexico. Citizen researchers, journalists and historians have explored how archival records — like those of the DFS or military reports released through transparency responses — intersect with popular narratives about unidentified aerial phenomena.

However, institutional responses consistently position these archives as **records of inquiry and reporting practices**, not as confirmation of extraterrestrial phenomena. The presence of UFO‑related references in archives reflects **official curiosity and documentation of civilian interest**, rather than hidden evidence of alien visitation.

In summary, Mexico’s archives and transparency records offer a structured window into how unexplained aerial reports are recorded, stored and released by government institutions. They provide valuable historical and procedural context for researchers, while remaining anchored in **legally accessible documentation** that emphasises **public accountability, not proof of extraterrestrial contact**.
