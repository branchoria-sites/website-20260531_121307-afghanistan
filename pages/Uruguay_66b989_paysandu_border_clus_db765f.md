## Introduction

Western [Uruguay]({{ 'what-uruguays-ufo-files-really-show/' | relative_url }}) has become one of the country's most persistent UFO-reporting corridors, not because it has produced a single decisive case, but because the same broad zone keeps generating clusters of reports across decades. The departments of Paysandú, Salto and, to a lesser extent, Soriano sit along the Uruguay River frontier with Argentina, creating a setting where unusual lights can be observed simultaneously from multiple jurisdictions and then amplified through local media, tourism networks and social platforms. The result is a regional pattern that matters more than any individual sighting.

## Why the western border produces recurring clusters

Unlike isolated urban sightings, reports from western Uruguay often emerge in areas with long sightlines across rivers, agricultural plains and sparsely populated terrain. Witnesses in Paysandú and Salto frequently describe bright lights visible over large distances, which can make it difficult to determine altitude, speed or exact location.

The geography also creates a reporting multiplier. A light seen near the Uruguay River may be observed from both Uruguay and Argentina, generating multiple witness accounts that appear independent even when they refer to the same event. This cross-border visibility has become a recurring feature of regional UFO narratives and helps explain why some western Uruguay incidents receive wider attention than reports from other parts of the country. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.mercopress.com/2023/02/13/uruguayan-air-force-to-handle-ufo-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.mercopress.com">[MercoPress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.mercopress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uruguayan air force to handle ufo sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MercoPressUruguayan Air Force to handle UFO sightings13 Feb 2023 — Uruguay&#x27;s Air Force (FAU) is to investigate the sighting of strange re...</span></span></span>

Another factor is cultural memory. The border region already occupies a special place in Uruguay's UFO history because of the long-running La Aurora story near the Paysandú-Salto boundary. Even when modern sightings have no direct connection to that case, they occur in an area where residents are already familiar with UFO themes, making reports more likely to be discussed, documented and preserved. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.academia.edu/99223999/A_Unique_Argentinian_Ufological_Guru_of_the_1980s_The_Case_of_%C3%81ngel_Cristo_Acoglanis_Mount_Uritorco_and_the_Underground_City_of_Erks" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uruguay. ## The Termas de Almirón reports

### The 2023 wave of red-light sightings

One of the clearest modern examples of a western Uruguay sighting cluster emerged around Termas de Almirón in Paysandú during February 2023. Witnesses reported strange red lights in the sky, and videos quickly spread through social media networks. The event gained unusual visibility because it reportedly occurred during a local festival, creating a larger pool of observers than would normally be present in the area. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.mercopress.com/2023/02/13/uruguayan-air-force-to-handle-ufo-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.mercopress.com">[MercoPress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.mercopress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uruguayan air force to handle ufo sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MercoPressUruguayan Air Force to handle UFO sightings13 Feb 2023 — Uruguay&#x27;s Air Force (FAU) is to investigate the sighting of strange re...</span></span></span>

### Why the case attracted attention

The Almirón reports illustrate a recurring pattern in Uruguay's UFO history. The strongest public evidence consisted largely of witness testimony and videos of distant lights rather than close-range observations with clear physical traces. That limited the ability of investigators to determine what had occurred.

At the same time, the fact that military-linked investigators publicly acknowledged the reports gave the story greater credibility in the eyes of many observers. This distinction is important. CRIDOVNI's involvement demonstrated that the reports were considered worth examining, but it did not constitute confirmation of an extraordinary explanation. The Almirón case became part of the western Uruguay UFO narrative precisely because it occupied that ambiguous middle ground between ordinary misidentification and unresolved observation. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.mercopress.com/2023/02/13/uruguayan-air-force-to-handle-ufo-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.mercopress.com">[MercoPress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.mercopress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uruguayan air force to handle ufo sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MercoPressUruguayan Air Force to handle UFO sightings13 Feb 2023 — Uruguay&#x27;s Air Force (FAU) is to investigate the sighting of strange re...</span></span></span>

## Cross-border witnesses and regional spread

The western Uruguay corridor differs from many other national UFO hotspots because reports frequently cross administrative and national boundaries. Witnesses in Paysandú may share visual access to the same horizon as residents of Entre Ríos Province in Argentina. This can produce unusually large witness pools without necessarily providing better evidence.

In some cases, cross-border reports strengthen a sighting's credibility because they reduce the likelihood that all observers are repeating a single rumour. Independent accounts from different locations can help establish that something unusual was genuinely seen. However, they do not automatically establish what was seen. A distant aircraft, atmospheric phenomenon or astronomical object can generate the same pattern of geographically dispersed reports.

The 2023 Almirón wave reflected this dynamic. Reports circulated from both sides of the Uruguay River, and media coverage emphasised that witnesses in Uruguay and Argentina had described similar lights. The resulting narrative quickly expanded beyond Paysandú itself and became a regional story. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.mercopress.com/2023/02/13/uruguayan-air-force-to-handle-ufo-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.mercopress.com">[MercoPress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.mercopress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uruguayan air force to handle ufo sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MercoPressUruguayan Air Force to handle UFO sightings13 Feb 2023 — Uruguay&#x27;s Air Force (FAU) is to investigate the sighting of strange re...</span></span></span>

This pattern also helps explain why western Uruguay occupies a disproportionate place in the country's UFO memory. The region functions as a communications bridge linking local radio, provincial newspapers, Argentine audiences and tourism communities. Stories that might remain local elsewhere can travel rapidly across the border landscape.

The relationship is therefore circular. Historic reports helped create a regional UFO identity, and that identity now influences how contemporary sightings are received, remembered and publicised.

## How social media changes modern sightings

The difference between twentieth-century and twenty-first-century reporting in western Uruguay is not necessarily the number of lights being seen. It is the speed with which reports spread.

Earlier cases depended on newspapers, local investigators or word of mouth. Today, videos recorded on mobile phones can circulate nationally within hours.
