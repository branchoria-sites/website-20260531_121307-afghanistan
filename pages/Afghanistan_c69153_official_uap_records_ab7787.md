## Introduction

Official releases of government **Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) records** have transformed public access to previously restricted military and intelligence files. For [Afghanistan]({{ 'afghanistan/' | relative_url }}), this release context matters because some of the U.S. Central Command’s archived sensor footage and related records include sightings captured over or near Afghanistan during coalition operations. Understanding what the official releases *actually show* – and what they *do not* prove – requires navigating U.S. government declassification drives, the architecture of public records such as the **Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE)**, and how offices like the **All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)** handle regional cases within broader U.S. data collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...</span></span></span>(#endnote-1 "Snippet: U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...")

## The U.S. Transparency Initiative: PURSUE and AARO Records

### How Official UAP Files Are Being Released

In 2026, the United States government launched a coordinated effort to **review, declassify, and publish UAP-related documents and media** spanning decades of military, intelligence, and aerospace records. This initiative is codified under the **Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE)**, a multi‑agency program that publishes materials on a dedicated government portal hosted at war.gov/ufo. These materials include videos, photographs, intelligence reports, and written records released in tranches in May 2026.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...</span></span></span>(#endnote-1 "Snippet: U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...")

The **All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)** is the lead Department of War (formerly Defense) entity coordinating the review and public release of UAP records, alongside partners such as the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, NASA, the FBI and other agencies. AARO also maintains an official records hub that links to historical holdings in the National Archives and declassification guidance.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://uapradar.com/official-records/aaro.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapradar.com">[UAP Radar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapradar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Radar AARO Records | UAP Radar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Radar AARO Records | UAP Radar</span></span></span>

These releases represent **raw or lightly redacted official records**, not settled investigative conclusions. AARO and related officials emphasise that access does *not* equate to validated UAP explanations or evidence of non‑terrestrial technology. Many materials remain unresolved due to limited data, classification constraints, or missing corroborating context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...</span></span></span>(#endnote-1 "Snippet: U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...")

## Afghanistan Within Official UAP Records

### CENTCOM and Afghanistan‑Linked Footage

Among the tranches of U.S. UAP files are declassified records associated with the **United States Central Command (CENTCOM)** area of responsibility, which encompasses Afghanistan and surrounding regions. While no single official record asserts a confirmed extraordinary explanation for a phenomenon *in Afghanistan*, some released items are geographically tied to Afghan airspace or nearby operations.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.uapdisclosure.info/documents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapdisclosure.info">[UAP Disclosure Tracker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapdisclosure.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## Interpreting Official Records: Why ‘Unresolved’ ≠ Extraordinary

### The Limits of Public Releases

Official UAP records – especially those involving footage and limited metadata – often show phenomena that are visually ambiguous but lack comprehensive analytical data. This means:

* The official archive lets the public see *what was recorded*, but not *why it was recorded or what it categorically represents*.
* The U.S. government’s transparency initiative focuses on improving accessibility, not asserting scientific conclusions about extraterrestrial origins.
* Labels like “unidentified” consistently indicate an absence of positive identification, not evidence of a non‑terrestrial or anomalous craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...</span></span></span>(#endnote-1 "Snippet: U.S. Department of WarPresidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP...The Department of War just released the second tranche of re...")
