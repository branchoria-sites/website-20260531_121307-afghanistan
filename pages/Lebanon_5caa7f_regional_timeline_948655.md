## Introduction

Lebanon’s UFO timeline is not defined by one famous national incident. Instead, the country’s reports are scattered across different regions, decades and social settings: mountain villages in the Chouf district, coastal cities such as Beirut and Tripoli, and smaller communities in Mount Lebanon and the Bekaa Valley. Most reports come from civilian witnesses rather than official investigations, and many remain anecdotal. Yet when viewed as a timeline, they reveal changing patterns in how unusual aerial phenomena have been observed, interpreted and recorded.

## Early and Archived Reports

### The 1950s: Sparse Documentation Around Beirut

Claims of UFO sightings in Lebanon reach back to the early Cold War period, with later retellings referring to a reported Beirut sighting in 1952. However, documentation from this era is extremely limited, and most references appear in retrospective UFO compilations rather than contemporary official [records]({{ 'records-a21934/' | relative_url }}). As a result, these accounts occupy the lowest tier of evidential reliability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/mixfmlebanon1044/posts/first-documented-ufo-sighting-in-lebanon-beirut-1952/10155942082762353/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first documented ufo sighting in lebanon beirut 1952</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">First documented UFO sighting in Lebanon. What is notable is that Beirut already appeared as the focal point of many early stories. This reflects a pattern seen throughout Lebanese UFO history: reports tend to emerge from places with larger populations, stronger media coverage and greater opportunities for witnesses to share accounts publicly.

### The 1970s: The Baakline Case in Mount Lebanon

The most frequently cited historical Lebanese UFO case occurred on 4 August 1972 in the Baakline area of the Chouf district, southeast of Beirut. According to accounts preserved by UFO researchers, several members of one family observed a bright red object moving rapidly across the evening sky. Witnesses reportedly viewed it through binoculars and described unusual movement before it disappeared from sight.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/1972-ufo-over-lebanon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1972 ufo over lebanon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1972: UFO Over LebanonJuly 21, 2013 — Summary/Description: Mr. Walter Hamady, a professor of art, was one of several w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 21, 2013</span></span></span>

The significance of the Baakline report is not that it proved anything extraordinary. Rather, it became one of the few Lebanese sightings to enter international UFO literature. The event was discussed in publications associated with the Aerial Phenomena Research Organization (APRO), giving it wider visibility than most Lebanese reports.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/1972-ufo-over-lebanon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1972 ufo over lebanon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1972: UFO Over LebanonJuly 21, 2013 — Summary/Description: Mr. Walter Hamady, a professor of art, was one of several w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 21, 2013</span></span></span>

From a regional perspective, the case fits a recurring pattern in Lebanon: mountain environments often provide darker skies and longer viewing opportunities than urban centres, making unusual lights more noticeable. At the same time, those conditions can make distance and scale difficult to judge accurately.

## The 1980s and 1990s: A Quiet Period in the Record

Compared with neighbouring decades, the publicly accessible record contains relatively few Lebanese UFO reports from the 1980s and 1990s. This does not necessarily indicate fewer sightings. Instead, it reflects the absence of organised reporting networks and the difficulties of preserving local accounts.

Lebanon’s civil war and the political instability that followed also complicate interpretation. During periods of conflict, aircraft activity, military operations and public attention were focused elsewhere. Many unusual aerial observations may never have entered any searchable archive.

For researchers examining Lebanese UFO history, this gap is important. It highlights how the timeline is shaped not only by what people saw, but by whether those observations were recorded and preserved.

## Beirut, Tripoli, Zahlé and Coastal Cases

### Beirut and Mount Lebanon in the 2000s

As internet reporting became easier, sightings from Beirut and surrounding suburbs began appearing more regularly in online UFO databases. Reports from areas such as Tabariss and Zouk Mkayel described luminous objects, circular lights and occasionally triangular formations visible at night. ### Northern Lebanon and Tripoli

Northern Lebanon appears intermittently in UFO databases and social-media discussions. Reports from the Tripoli region typically involve bright lights, unusual motion or clusters of objects observed above the coast.

The geography matters. Northern Lebanon combines urban development with open views across the Mediterranean and mountainous inland terrain. Witnesses may be observing aircraft, satellites, atmospheric effects or distant military activity from neighbouring states. Because the region sits near several international air and maritime corridors, distinguishing unusual objects from conventional activity can be difficult.

Most northern Lebanese reports therefore remain unresolved rather than compellingly anomalous. The available evidence is generally too limited to support stronger conclusions.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.usufocenter.com/ufo-sighting-reports/worldwide/lebanon-ufo-sightings.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usufocenter.com">[US UFO Research Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usufocenter.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">more...</span></span></span>

### Zahlé and the Bekaa Valley

The Bekaa Valley, including the Zahlé area, appears occasionally in online sighting records from the late 2000s and early 2010s. Witnesses typically reported bright objects, hovering lights or unusual movements visible against the valley's open night skies.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.usufocenter.com/ufo-sighting-reports/worldwide/lebanon-ufo-sightings.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usufocenter.com">[US UFO Research Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usufocenter.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">more...</span></span></span>

Compared with Beirut, reports from the Bekaa often came from less densely populated settings where artificial light pollution is lower. This can improve visibility but also increase the likelihood that astronomical objects appear unfamiliar. Planets, [meteors]({{ 'meteors/' | relative_url }}) and satellite passes frequently feature in later explanations for similar reports worldwide.

The available Lebanese record does not show a distinctive Bekaa-specific UFO pattern. Instead, the region contributes another cluster of scattered observations within the national timeline.

## The 2020s: Orb Reports and Renewed Interest

### Repeated Sightings Near Beirut

The most sustained cluster of recent Lebanese UFO reports comes from 2024–2025 observations logged with the National UFO Reporting Center. Reports from the Bchaaleh area and nearby parts of Mount Lebanon described recurring orb-like lights appearing over extended periods.
