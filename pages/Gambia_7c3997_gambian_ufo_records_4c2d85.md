## Introduction

In The [Gambia]({{ 'what-is-really-behind-gambias-ufo/' | relative_url }}), **there is no official, publicly accessible national archive dedicated to unidentified flying objects (UFOs) or unidentified aerial phenomena (UAPs)**. Unlike countries with formalised UFO files — such as the UK’s Ministry of Defence records at The National Archives — there is no known Gambian government office that systematically records, classifies or publishes unusual aerial event reports under a UFO/UAP archive. What does exist in The Gambia are **standard aviation safety and regulatory records**, maintained by civil aviation authorities and regional safety bodies, and occasional **media accounts of anomalous sightings or objects**, which remain largely informal and anecdotal. This page explains why an obvious Gambian UFO archive is “missing”, where relevant information might actually appear, and how aviation reporting structures shape what is and isn’t documented.

## Why There Is No Clear National UFO Archive in The Gambia

The absence of a dedicated UFO archive in The Gambia reflects how aerial observation and reporting are institutionally structured:

* **Civil aviation authorities do not collect UFO‑specific reports.** The **Gambia Civil Aviation Authority** (GCAA) is responsible for regulating civil aviation in The Gambia, including aircraft operations, air traffic services, licensing, airworthiness, and safety oversight. It operates under international conventions like the Chicago Convention and provides publications and procedures relating to aviation safety, but its online documents and publications do not reference anomalies labelled as UFOs or UAPs. It focuses on aeronautical information, flight safety standards, obstacle control, and air navigation services rather than unexplained aerial sightings. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gcaa.aero/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gcaa.aero">[gcaa.aero]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gcaa.aero</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Gambia Civil Aviation Authority – GCAA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Gambia Civil Aviation Authority – GCAA</span></span></span>
* **Search and rescue and aviation reporting are structured around safety, not mystery.** The GCAA’s Air Navigation Services include air traffic control, aeronautical information services and search and rescue functions aimed at protecting lives and aircraft, not compiling UFO cases. Records that might include unusual aerial sightings (e.g. flight plans, NOTAMs) are generated in the context of routine aviation operations, not UFO investigations. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gcaa.aero/gcaa-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gcaa.aero">[gcaa.aero]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gcaa.aero</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GCA A Documents – The Gambia Civil Aviation Authority</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Airport, Yundum, GM * Directorates * Air Transport * Flight Safety Standards * Publications * ANS * eAIP-Gambia *...</span></span></span>
* **No government‑mandated UFO policy exists.** Unlike some national archives that accumulated UFO files due to historical military or public inquiries (e.g. the UK’s Ministry of Defence UFO reports preserved at The National Archives), The Gambia has no identifiable internal policy to store correspondence, sightings or public files under a UFO category. Searches for dedicated Gambia UFO reporting databases return third‑party sites that compile user‑submitted reports, but these are not official records. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.usufocenter.com/ufo-sighting-reports/worldwide/gambia-ufo-sightings.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usufocenter.com">[usufocenter.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usufocenter.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Global UFOs: Gambia UFO Sightings &amp; ReportsWe are working as quickly as possible to add all received Gambia UFO and alien contact experie...</span></span></span>

**In practical terms, this means what researchers might call “UFO archives” are not missing out of malice or secrecy — they simply were never systematically built or institutionalised in the first place.**

## How Aviation Incident Reporting Changes the Trail

Although there is no UFO archive, several established records and reporting mechanisms can nonetheless intersect with unusual aerial phenomena:

* **Aviation incident databases and safety plans can contain relevant data.** The national **Gambia National Aviation Safety Plan 2026–2028** envisages publishing annual safety reports, including accident and serious incident summaries, on the GCAA website. While such reports are focused on aircraft and safety performance, unusual aerial events that affect safety (e.g. unidentified objects entering controlled airspace) *could* appear within those technical occurrence records if reported through formal channels. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/safety/GASP/GASP%20Library/National%20aviation%20safety%20plans/Gambia-NASP-2026-2028-Revised.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icao.int">[ICAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icao.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gambia NASP 2026 2028 Revised</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ICAOTHE GAMBIA NATIONALDecember 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 23, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Regional aviation safety systems record operational anomalies.** The **BAGASOO** (Banjul Accord Group Aviation Safety & Security Oversight Organisation) promotes harmonised safety oversight across several West African states including The Gambia. Member states’ safety information management systems can capture incident data, although again focused on safety and compliance, not UFO phenomena per se. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.bagasoo.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bagasoo.org">[bagasoo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bagasoo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">BAGASO O</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGASO O</span></span></span>

* **Aviation safety incident logs and annual safety reports.** Technical documents produced by the GCAA or through regional safety bodies may include descriptions of events where aircraft, radar or air traffic controllers encountered unidentified objects or unexplained sensor returns. These are typically anonymised but can provide factual data about anomalies.
* **Local and regional media reports of unusual objects.** While anecdotal, Gambian media items and social posts (e.g. community reports of objects falling from the sky) offer primary leads that can be cross‑checked against aviation records or satellite data. ## Summary Takeaways for the Reader


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* There is **no well‑defined Gambian UFO archive** maintained by government agencies; any claims about “missing files” must be understood in the context that **such archives were never formally created**.
* Aviation records that do exist in The Gambia, managed by the GCAA and regional safety organisations, focus on **aviation safety and regulatory compliance**, not unidentified phenomena.
* Researchers interested in unexplained aerial events in The Gambia must rely on **aviation incident data**, **media anecdotes**, and **global UFO reporting databases** as proxies, recognising the limitations of each.

</div>

The “missing” UFO archive is not a gap caused by concealment but by structural absence: Gambian institutions record aircraft and safety incidents, not unexplained aerial phenomena, and there’s currently **no transparent system in place to collect or publish UFO/UAP files** under that designation.
