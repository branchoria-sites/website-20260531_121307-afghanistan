## Introduction

[Spain]({{ 'spains-ufo-files-mystery-evidence-and/' | relative_url }})’s declassified Air Force UFO archive is often presented as evidence that the government secretly confirmed extraordinary aerial phenomena. The documents themselves tell a different story. What the archive proves most clearly is that the Spanish military investigated unusual reports, preserved records, and sometimes could not reach a definitive conclusion. It does not prove extraterrestrial visitors, hidden alien technology, or a long-running cover-up.

That distinction matters because Spain possesses one of Europe’s most accessible official UFO collections. The archive contains around 80 declassified files covering 122 incidents between 1962 and 1995, amounting to roughly 1,900 pages of reports, witness statements, radar references, technical assessments, and administrative correspondence. The files are valuable because they allow readers to examine how military investigators handled uncertainty rather than relying only on rumours or sensational retellings. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Virtual Defensa Expedientes OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblioteca Virtual DefensaExpedientes OVNI - Biblioteca Virtual de DefensaSe trata de un total de 80 expedientes, 1.900 páginas de avista...</span></span></span>

## How Spain Declassified Its UFO Records

Spain’s Ministry of Defence began a formal declassification process in the early 1990s. Rather than releasing a few headline cases, the Air Force gradually opened an entire body of files relating to what it called “strange phenomena” observed in Spanish airspace or involving Air Force personnel and equipment. The collection was later digitised and placed in the Ministry’s Virtual Defence Library, making it unusually accessible compared with many European military archives. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Virtual Defensa Expedientes OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblioteca Virtual DefensaExpedientes OVNI - Biblioteca Virtual de DefensaSe trata de un total de 80 expedientes, 1.900 páginas de avista...</span></span></span>

One reason the archive attracts attention is that Spain released the material without claiming that the cases demonstrated alien visitation. The Ministry treated the files as historical records rather than proof of extraordinary events. That administrative attitude is important when interpreting what the archive actually shows.

## What the 80 Files Contain

The collection covers reports from across Spain rather than from a single famous hotspot. Cases include sightings near Barcelona, the Canary Islands, Madrid, the Balearic Sea, Seville, Alicante, Galicia, Girona, Asturias, Navarra, military radar stations, and commercial flights. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Virtual Defensa Expedientes OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblioteca Virtual DefensaExpedientes OVNI - Biblioteca Virtual de DefensaSe trata de un total de 80 expedientes, 1.900 páginas de avista...</span></span></span>

Several broad categories appear repeatedly.

### Aviation and Airspace Incidents

Many reports involved pilots, radar operators, air-defence personnel, or aircraft crews. These cases naturally received more attention because they affected controlled airspace or flight safety.

Examples include:

* The Palma-to-Madrid flight incident of February 1969.
* The famous [Manises]({{ 'manises/' | relative_url }}) incident of November 1979, in which a commercial airliner diverted after the crew reported unexplained lights.
* Reports linked to radar surveillance installations known as EVA stations.
* Encounters reported from military bases and training areas. ### Canary Islands Cases

The Canary Islands occupy a special place in Spanish UFO history. Multiple archive files concern dramatic luminous phenomena observed over the Atlantic between the 1970s and early 1990s. Some incidents involved large numbers of witnesses and generated national media attention. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Virtual Defensa Expedientes OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblioteca Virtual DefensaExpedientes OVNI - Biblioteca Virtual de DefensaSe trata de un total de 80 expedientes, 1.900 páginas de avista...</span></span></span>

## Why “Unidentified” Does Not Mean “Alien”

The archive repeatedly demonstrates a common misunderstanding in UFO discussions: an unidentified object is not automatically an extraterrestrial object.

Military investigators often used a process of elimination. They examined available information and attempted to match a report to known causes. When evidence remained insufficient, the case sometimes stayed unresolved. That outcome simply meant investigators lacked enough information for a confident identification. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/28130360/UFO_Declassification_The_Spanish_Model" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Declassification The Spanish Model</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) UFO Declassification: The Spanish Model18 Jan 2021 — Brief history of author&#x27;s participation in the process of declassifica...</span></span></span>

This distinction becomes clearer when examining the archive’s unresolved cases. Some files remain officially unexplained because:

* Radar data were incomplete.
* No physical evidence existed.
* Atmospheric conditions were uncertain.
* Too much time had passed before investigation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/35786573/Spanish_Air_Force_UFO_Files_The_Secrets_End_pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spanish Air Force UFO Files The Secrets End pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spanish Air Force UFO Files:The Secret&#x27;s End.pdfIt will detail the Spanish Air Force&#x27; s involvement in the UFO subject from 1968 to prese...</span></span></span>

## The Canary Islands: A Lesson in Misidentifying Rare Events

No section of the archive better illustrates this point than the Canary Islands cases.

The spectacular 22 June 1976 incident attracted enormous attention. Witnesses across the islands reported luminous forms and unusual aerial displays. ## What Spain’s UFO Files Actually Prove

The strongest conclusions supported by the archive are more limited than many enthusiasts hope, but they are also more defensible.

The files prove that:

* Spanish military personnel recorded and investigated unusual aerial reports over several decades.
* Some incidents involved credible witnesses, including pilots, radar operators, and military staff.
* Certain cases remained unresolved after investigation.
* Official uncertainty was preserved rather than erased.
* Many reports eventually acquired conventional explanations or plausible non-extraordinary interpretations.
