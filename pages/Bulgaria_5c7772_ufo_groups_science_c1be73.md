## Introduction

In the context of Bulgaria’s ongoing public fascination with sightings and unexplained aerial phenomena, a distinct landscape of local enthusiasts, clubs, community networks, and occasional scientific engagement has developed around UFO (Unidentified Flying Object) reports. Unlike countries with formal government programmes or extensive academic research on the topic, Bulgaria’s UFO investigation activity has mainly thrived at the grassroots level: hobbyist groups, individual researchers, and occasional interactions with scientific institutions. At the same time, attempts to bring scientific scrutiny to these phenomena have met with mixed responses, limited formal study, and scepticism from mainstream science. This page examines who investigates UFO claims in Bulgaria, how these efforts are structured, and where scientific scrutiny intersects (or fails to) with popular UFO interest in the country.

## Grassroots Clubs and Enthusiast Networks

One of the longest‑standing strands of UFO interest in Bulgaria has been the formation of local clubs and networks aimed at sharing reports, organising meetings and, unofficially, investigating sightings.

* **UFO Disclosure Bulgaria** is a prominent private initiative. Founded and led by Ivan Ivanov, it positions itself as a community and investigation group, conducting field enquiries, interviewing witnesses, and producing documentaries and online content about unexplained aerial phenomena and related paranormal reports. These groups operate largely outside formal institutional frameworks. Their activities range from collecting eyewitness reports to hosting meetings and online forums where members share images, personal experiences, and interpretations. However, they generally lack access to specialised instrumentation, peer‑reviewed methodology, or formal scientific validation, aligning them more with hobbyist ufology than structured scientific inquiry.

## Interfaces with Scientific Institutions

By contrast with grassroots networks, formal scientific institutions in Bulgaria have historically been cautious about engaging with UFO claims as a serious research domain, reflecting wider international scepticism regarding UFO investigation as a scientific discipline.

* In 2014, the **Bulgarian Academy of Sciences** (BAS) Institute for Space Studies convened an official conference to discuss the existence of unidentified flying phenomena and extraterrestrial intelligence, acknowledging the public interest and debating how to approach witness claims. The deputy director noted the desire to form an internal working group to evaluate case reports systematically.[Novinite]
* Reports from that period suggested Bulgarian scientists, including BAS researchers, had at times been quoted in the media claiming extraterrestrial life was present on Earth — interpretations that were widely contested and regarded sceptically by the broader scientific community. It is unclear whether these claims reflected mainstream BAS policy or fringe statements amplified by tabloid coverage.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/worldnews/europe/bulgaria/6650677/Aliens-already-exist-on-earth-Bulgarian-scientists-claim.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraph.co.uk">[The Telegraph]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraph.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens already exist on earth Bulgarian scientists claim</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The TelegraphAliens &#x27;already exist on earth&#x27;, Bulgarian scientists claim26 Nov 2009 — Aliens from outer space are already among us on ear...</span></span></span>

Despite these episodes, formal scientific engagement in Bulgaria has been sporadic and limited. The Academy’s principal research bodies such as the **Space Research and Technology Institute** and observatories are focused on established space science, remote sensing, astrophysics and astronautics, rather than UFO phenomena. Scientific institutions typically treat UFO sightings as cultural or psychological phenomena, or as cases of misidentification that require rigorous atmospheric, astronomical, or technical analysis rather than extraordinary explanations.

## Why Scientific Scrutiny Remains Limited

The contrast between active UFO enthusiasts and the formal scientific establishment in Bulgaria reflects broader international divides over UFO study.

* **Methodological barriers:** Scientific inquiry requires reproducibility, controlled observation, and falsifiable hypotheses. Unidentified aerial reports tend to be isolated, non‑reproducible events based on eyewitness testimony. This makes them difficult to investigate with methodologies accepted in mainstream physics and space science. Ufology, as a term and field of study, is widely regarded outside of official science as lacking rigorous, falsifiable frameworks.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* **Institutional priorities:** Bulgarian scientific institutions, like their counterparts elsewhere, prioritise established research agendas (e.g. astrophysics, aerospace technologies) and have limited resources for exploratory research on anomalous sightings. While individual scientists may express interest or engage with public discourse, sustained institutional programmes dedicated to UFO investigation are absent.
* **Public and scientific perception:** UFO interest often overlaps with fringe theories and paranormal interpretations, which can discourage serious scientific engagement. In many cases, scientists view anomalous sightings as explainable natural or man‑made phenomena awaiting clarification through conventional research methods.

## Conclusion

In Bulgaria, the landscape of UFO investigation comprises active grassroots communities of hobbyists and enthusiasts sharing reports, forums and occasional symposiums, alongside limited and intermittent scientific attention from established research institutions. While groups such as UFO Disclosure Bulgaria and regional networks maintain public interest and gather anecdotal evidence, the absence of robust scientific structures dedicated to UAP (Unidentified Aerial Phenomena) investigation means that most inquiries remain informal. Scientific scrutiny tends to frame these reports within broader research contexts — astronomical, atmospheric, perceptual — rather than as evidence of extraordinary phenomena. This tension between popular UFO interest and formal scientific scepticism shapes how UFO claims are investigated and understood within Bulgarian society today.
